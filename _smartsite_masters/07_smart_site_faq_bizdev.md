# Smart Site FAQ

## The product

**What is Smart Site in one sentence?**
A smart site is everything you need to know about a place, in one place: the property lines, what you can build, the setbacks, the flood risk, the terrain, the codes, the utilities, on one map, current and cited.

**Who is it for?**
Professionals who analyze property for a living: agents and brokers, architects, investors, developers, land planners, civil consultants. The wedge is the same for all of them: be the most informed person in the room, and hand over a cited analysis instead of an opinion.

**What does it actually tell me that the county GIS doesn't?**
The county GIS tells you where the lines are. Smart Site tells you what they mean: the buildable envelope actually drawn after setbacks, the flood implication for this specific lot, the code section behind the number, each answer cited, dated, and carrying a confidence level. It is the difference between a data lookup and an answer you can act on and defend.

**What is the X-ray?**
The deep-read report on one smart site. Not a folder of documents; a read through the surface to every layer underneath: what you can build with the envelope drawn, flood and drainage, terrain, codes, utilities, each sourced and dated. It is the artifact a realtor shares with a client, an architect hands a reviewer, a developer runs before buying.

**What other reports are there?**
Flood and Drainage is the other live report. Feasibility and Comparison are reports on the locked menu and are not live generate paths today. Comparison is also the side-by-side tool. Brief is the inspect dock. Records is a courthouse request. Do not pitch a report that is not on the live card.

**Is this a valuation tool?**
No, deliberately. Smart Site tells you what you can do with a place, not what it is worth. Constraints and buildability, cited, is the wedge; valuation is a crowded, opinion-driven category and we stake the brand on being right about facts instead.

**Wasn't this called Property Explorer?**
Yes; the product was renamed Smart Site. The new name is the positioning: the unit and the product are one word, so a professional can say "I'll share your smart site with you" and be understood instantly. Property Explorer survives only in historical records.

## Coverage and data

**What's your coverage?**
Nationwide United States. Coverage is produced by a factory pipeline that onboards jurisdictions in parallel, and every jurisdiction passes mechanical certification gates before it serves. If a buyer asks about a specific place, confirm their jurisdiction on request rather than reciting a list.

**Where does the data come from?**
Public authoritative records: county appraisal and GIS parcels, city zoning ordinances and development codes, FEMA flood panels, state lidar terrain, hydrography, and licensed code libraries where applicable. Every fact carries its source; that is the point.

**How current is it?**
Currency is checked, not assumed. The system cross-checks a code's claimed edition against the jurisdiction's own adoption record, detects repealed ordinances still published as current, and catches re-platted parcels whose old records still resolve. Where a fact cannot be verified current, the system says so instead of serving it.

**What happens where you don't have data?**
The product says "not verified here," names what is missing, and never fills the gap with a guess. This is a feature to sell, not a gap to apologize for: a system that tells you what it doesn't know is the reason you can trust what it does. Also worth knowing: much unincorporated land in many states is legitimately unzoned; an honest "no zoning applies" is the correct answer there, not missing data.

**Is it accurate enough to build on?**
It is accurate enough to decide with, and honest about its precision. Envelopes are labeled approximate, not survey grade; every export says derived from public records, not a boundary survey, not for legal record, verify with city staff and licensed professionals. We are the fastest defensible read, not a replacement for a stamped survey or a permit.

## The AI

**How do I know the AI isn't making things up?**
Three structural guarantees. The assistant can only cite records that actually exist for that property; the format makes an invented citation impossible. Every citation opens: claim, source, confidence, date, and the reasoning chain behind it. And where there is no record, the assistant says so in plain prose rather than inventing an answer. The chat is anchored to the property; there is no free-floating chatbot to wander off the facts.

**What's the confidence score?**
Every answer carries a confidence signal with its basis stated. The system never shows a bare number dressed up as more than it is; where confidence is asserted from source quality rather than earned from outcomes, it says "asserted." The calibration loop that tightens confidence with real-world use is part of the architecture.

## Trust and privacy

**Who owns my data?**
You do. The rule is structural: public information is public and everyone benefits from it; your private information, your saved properties, your research, your attached documents, stays private to you and whomever you choose to share it with. Private data is never pooled into a shared or public asset, and never mined to serve someone else.

**Does my research train your models?**
No. The reasoning layer improves from public-tier and anonymous signal only. Tenant-private data and adjudications are excluded from any shared learning by access policy enforced at the data layer, not by a promise in a contract.

## Competition

(Reminder: never attack a competitor by name in customer material. The posture below is for conversations, framed as what we are rather than what they are not.)

**"I already have a GIS/parcel map app."**
Parcel viewers show you where the lines are. Smart Site computes what they mean: the envelope drawn, the flood implication, the code behind the number, cited. And where a viewer silently shows stale or missing data, Smart Site verifies currency and declines honestly.

**"I already have a data subscription for property research."**
Data vendors sell rows; the reconciliation is left to you. Smart Site's entire value is the assembly: incompatible records joined to one place, conflicts reconciled by a defensible rule, staleness caught, and the answer computed, with receipts. That is the unsolved hard part, and it's what you're paying for.

**"Can't I just ask ChatGPT?"**
General AI guesses about the physical world because it has no verified source to consult; it will confidently produce a setback that does not exist. Smart Site's assistant is the opposite construction: it can only cite verified records for the property in question, and says so when there is none. When AI agents do this work at scale, they will need exactly this verified channel, which is part of why we built it.

**"The county gives this away free."**
So do we, at the browse layer. The county gives you fragments across a dozen systems; we give you the assembled answer, and the free layer is genuinely free. The paid product is the deep work: the X-ray, the studies, the exports, unlimited cited research.

## Commercial

**What does it cost?**
Free to browse, genuinely — the map, the inspect card, saved properties, three AI chat messages per property, and sharing. Solo at $49/mo is the full answer on a parcel: the X-ray, the Flood and Drainage study, unlimited chat, unlimited properties. Studio at $129/mo adds the professional deliverables: site plan CAD (DXF and IFC), terrain export, and owner data. Team is $299/mo for up to 3 seats, then $25 a seat. A single property can also be unlocked for $15 for 30 days. One unlock moment, never a wall per button.

**Why is the unlock 30 days and not forever?**
Because the answer has a shelf life. Bastrop repealed its entire zoning code, and any product promising a permanent answer would have been serving the dead version. The bound is a freshness guarantee, not a meter: what you get is an answer verified on a date, and the date is on it. It is the on-ramp — a little over three properties and Solo is the better buy, which is exactly the point.

**Why does owner data sit in Studio rather than Solo?**
Skip-trace is a professional capability, not a $49 impulse feature. Studio is the tier for people who produce something they hand to someone else — drawings, exports, an owner approach — so the deliverables travel together.

**Is there a team/brokerage offer?**
Yes, and it closes without talking to anyone: $299/mo covers up to 3 seats, then $25 per additional seat, with shared saved properties and one bill. A firm swipes a card and invites colleagues. Extra seats stay monthly. There are no negotiated terms to improvise, by design.

**Can I talk to someone / get a demo?**
The product is the demo. The inspect card is free, needs no login, and answers a real parcel in seconds. There is no sales call because there is no sales team on this product — the pricing page answers the questions a rep would, and anything it fails to answer is a defect in the page, not a reason to book a meeting. (Custom builds and municipal deployments are different businesses and do have people; that is Empressa Solutions and SmartCity OS, not Smart Site.)

**Can cities buy this?**
Cities are a different product: SmartCity OS, a whole community of smart sites run as one operating picture, with its own positioning and sales motion. Hand a municipal conversation to that track; never sell a city from Smart Site professional collateral.

**Can software agents or platforms consume this?**
Architecturally yes, and it is a strategic direction: the same answers, citations, and confidence exposed programmatically, metered. Speak of it as architecture and direction; commercial agent access is arranged conversation by conversation, not self-serve today.

## Objections to expect

**"How do I know this is right?"**
Open any citation. Then show honest absence somewhere: the product admitting what it doesn't know is the fastest trust-builder in a demo.

**"What if it's wrong and my client relies on it?"**
Every output is cited to its source and carries the verification disclaimer; the professional's judgment stays in the loop, now with receipts. The honest answer: we are dramatically better than the status quo (hand-assembled fragments of unknown currency), and we are honest about the boundary of what we verify.

**"This seems too cheap / too expensive."**
Too cheap: the price is an on-ramp; the business is Pro subscriptions and, over time, the layer itself. Too expensive: one avoided mistake, one won listing, one saved site-assembly day pays for a year.
