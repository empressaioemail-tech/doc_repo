# OPS-11 — The Invariant Register

## WHAT THIS IS

Four parallel invariant sets were running in this repo at once: the eight Geometry Laws ratified at the envelope saga close, the seven determinism invariants in OPS-3, the five design invariants in the Smart Site technical white paper, and the five non-negotiables at the top of the operator manual. Plus the four portfolio-level structural commitments in CLAUDE.md, which sit at a different altitude and stay a separate tier. They overlap, they restate each other in different words, and in at least one place they disagree about whether a rule is enforced. This register reconciles them into one list.

The rule that makes this register worth having: every invariant names the specific mechanism that makes its defect class unrepresentable, or it is marked UNENFORCED. An unenforced invariant is a wish. Naming it as a wish is the honest move, and quietly upgrading a wish into an invariant is exactly the drift this register exists to stop. The Geometry Law set is the model here, because every one of its eight laws was ratified with the mechanism that closed it.

Enforcement status values used below:

ENFORCED means a specific test, gate, or guard exists in live source and was located during this reconciliation. PARTIAL means the mechanism exists but does not cover the full statement, and the gap is named. UNENFORCED means no mechanism was found and the rule currently rests on operator discipline or prose. UNVERIFIED-IN-SOURCE means the source doc claims a check that could not be located in the repos read for this pass, which is a weaker claim than UNENFORCED because the check may exist under a name not searched.

Verification pass ran 2026-08-09 against `P:\hauska-engine` (main, tip `ed2498f`), `P:\legacy-design-tools`, `P:\hauska-map`, and `P:\doc_repo\hauska-mcp-server`. Every path cited below was read or grep-located in that pass.

## CONFLICTS AND DISCREPANCIES BETWEEN THE SOURCE SETS

Four things came out of this reconciliation that the source docs do not agree on, and one of them means a source doc is now wrong.

The first and most important: OPS-3 lists I2 as an open nondeterminism leak, naming `promote.ts:75` and `warm-compute.ts:116` as live `new Date().toISOString()` calls that put timestamps into the content hash. That is no longer true. `content-hash-determinism.test.ts` exists and asserts precisely the fix OPS-3 prescribes, including recursive stripping of nested provenance keys. Meanwhile `promote.ts:75` is now inside the doc comment for `EnvelopeWriteThenVerifyMismatchError`, not a timestamp call at all, and `warm-compute.ts:116` is inside an edge-label mapping function. The line numbers in OPS-3 have drifted past the code they cited. INV-10 records the enforced reality; OPS-3's leak-1 entry should be retired at the next edit of that doc.

The second: OPS-3 I1 states the no-LLM invariant is enforced by "a CI grep-gate on those paths." No such gate exists. `hauska-engine/.github/workflows/ci.yml` has exactly five substantive steps (install, python worker deps, hydrology tracer parity, typecheck, test) and no grep step. The property itself holds, verified grep-clean across `depth-warm`, `geometry`, and `warden` in this pass, but it holds as a fact about today's source rather than as a guarantee about tomorrow's. This is the single clearest example of the failure this register exists to catch: an invariant labeled "enforced, not aspirational" whose named enforcement does not exist. Building that grep step is the cheapest enforcement win on this list.

The third: the Smart Site technical white paper §1 closes with "These are not aspirations. Section 4 describes the gates and checks that enforce them." Section 4 does not do that. It describes one worked example, the buildable envelope pipeline, in five steps, and its step 5 enumerates the envelope's verification gates in real detail. It then asserts the pattern "generalizes across the reasoning outputs" to flood depth and compliance reads without naming a single gate for either. Of the five §1 invariants, §4 substantively covers invariant 2 (correctness verified) for one output family, touches invariant 3 (honest failure) in the setback-resolution step, and does not address invariants 1, 4, or 5 at all; currency is handled in §3 and access in §7, both outside the section §1 points at. The forward reference overstates what it delivers. This is a doc-accuracy defect in a diligence-facing artifact and should be either narrowed to "section 4 describes the envelope gates; sections 3 and 7 cover currency and access" or expanded to actually enumerate.

The fourth is a difference of altitude rather than a contradiction, worth naming so it is not mistaken for one. The Geometry Law set and the operator manual's five non-negotiables overlap heavily but enforce differently. Geometry Law 6 (conformance not templates) and operator rule 5 (area-sweep not sample) are the same defect class approached from opposite ends: the law bans the fossil answer key, the rule bans the curated roster. Both are needed, and INV-6 and INV-16 keep them separate because their mechanisms differ and INV-16's is weaker. Similarly, operator rule 3 (persisted equals recompute) and white paper §4's closing paragraph state the same thing, merged here as INV-15. Operator rule 4 and white paper invariant 3 are the same honest-failure rule, merged as INV-17.

## WHAT THIS REGISTER DOES NOT COVER

This is a register of invariants, not a register of correctness. An invariant with a green enforcing check means a defect class is structurally hard to reintroduce; it does not mean the system is correct today, and it does not mean the check is well-specified. The saga's own lesson applies here: engine #275 was a real labeling defect that passed the mechanical gate and was caught only by the operator's eye, which is why INV-25's human half is deliberately unenforced rather than a gap to close.

Scope limits worth stating plainly. The verification pass covered four repos and did not read `smartcity-os`, `cortex-api`, or the atom-contract source repo, so invariants whose enforcement lives there are under-verified, INV-19 and INV-20 most of all. Deploy, infrastructure, and CI-hygiene rules (the traffic trap, the image race, merge-only-on-green, migration-merged-not-applied) are operational traps in the standing-decisions block and the memory system, not invariants about what the machinery computes, and they are deliberately out of scope. Commercial and legal commitments are out of scope. Product-surface and brand-placement rules live in the repo-intent catalog and are out of scope.

Finally, this register is a snapshot with a date on it. Six of the twenty-five Tier 1 entries are PARTIAL specifically because a mechanism exists but does not cover the full statement, and three are UNENFORCED with no mechanism at all. Those nine are the work list. The register's value decays the moment source moves under it, which is exactly what happened to OPS-3's line-number citations, so the enforcing-check column should be re-verified against live source before any external claim rests on it.
