# Custom builds and property operations platforms

The "medium" in small/medium/large. This is the offering for businesses that own or operate property: property management companies, apartment owners (from one building to thousands of units), builders, brokerages, and really any business whose operations touch real property. Mox, a large Austin multifamily operator we are engaged with, is the reference shape of this engagement; the pitch below works for any company like them, at any size.

## One-liner

We build the intelligence layer underneath the systems you already run. One dashboard, all your properties and all your systems in one view, and your institutional knowledge finally compounds instead of evaporating.

## The problem

A property operator of any size runs on a patchwork: accounting in one system, leasing in another, maintenance in a third, utilities across six vendor logins, construction-side software, spreadsheets, and email. Each captures a piece of the business. None of them talk to each other. Answering one question means flipping through four screens. And what the team learns (which vendor is reliable, why that variance happened, what that renovation actually cost) lives in people's heads and evaporates with every staff transition or software change.

## What we build

**Connect first, replace later, if ever.** We integrate underneath the existing stack. No rip-and-replace, no two-year migration before anything works. Their systems stay in place, their teams keep the tools they know, and the intelligence layer unifies what those systems produce. Over time the customer decides which legacy systems stay and which become optional. Their call, their timeline.

**The property twin as the foundation.** Every property they own or manage goes on the map as a digital twin (this is the same base layer as everything else we sell). Their buildings, then their systems, attach to it: utilities and usage per unit, maintenance history, appliance service records, leases, work orders.

**One dashboard, many lenses.** The operator's dashboard becomes the surface their teams live in, role-scoped: accountants see the close, site managers see their property, owners see the portfolio. Existing systems become tabs, not destinations.

**You own everything.** Their data and their operating history are theirs on day one. If they ever leave, the data leaves with them in a usable format. This is the reversal of what they experience with every other vendor, and it lands hard with anyone who has ever tried to get their own data out of a property-management platform.

## What it does for them (pick by audience)

**For a property manager / owner-operator:**
- All utility accounts and operating costs in one view, tracked per unit.
- The building tells you when it is sick: maintenance patterns, aging appliances, rising usage flagged before they become emergencies. (Full sensor-driven monitoring is the roadmap; pattern intelligence on their existing data is the day-one story.)
- A clean tenant portal: one place for residents to pay, submit requests, and see notices.
- Service workers get the unit's full history on their phone when they arrive.

**For the capital side (owners, investors):**
- Underwrite the next acquisition against the real operating costs of the portfolio, not the seller's pro forma.
- Actuals versus underwrite visible continuously, not reconstructed at quarter end.
- When they sell or refinance, the buyer gets a complete structured operating record. The value they added is documented in data.

**For the construction side (builders, rehab arms):**
- Multi-jurisdiction code intelligence: setbacks, parking, accessibility, energy code, loaded and queryable for every market they build in.
- Pre-submission plan review: run the review the city will run before submitting, and skip resubmission cycles that each cost weeks of carrying cost.
- Scope, materials, cost, and subcontractor history from every prior job feeding the next bid.

## The savings story (how to say it honestly)

Do not promise a single number. The honest and effective framing: "It is ten percent here, thirty percent there, across a dozen workflows. Flattened logins, faster close, fewer resubmissions, problems caught before they are emergencies. We scope your specific savings in discovery, against your actual operations." If a prospect needs a number, that is a discovery conversation, not a claim.

## How the sale works

This is a discovery-first relationship sale, and it is Cammie's natural motion:

1. **Open with pain, not product.** "What are your bottlenecks? What do you wish was automated? What makes you flip through four screens?" The answers write the proposal.
2. **Start with whatever data they already have.** No big-bang 3D model required on day one. First win is usually something visible and clean (utilities in one view, monthly close, a tenant portal).
3. **Scope as a phased engagement.** Discovery, then a first shipped win the team can touch, then build-out across the operation. Every phase ships something usable.
4. **Custom collateral on request.** When Cammie identifies a specific prospect ("Bob owns three apartment buildings in Waco"), Nick and Valerie produce a tailored package for that prospect. Send who they are and what they do; a package comes back.

Anchor line for scale: the reference engagement shape is a multifamily operator with thousands of units across dozens of communities, and the same architecture scales down to a single 29-story building or a three-building portfolio. Nobody is too small if the pain is real.

## Talk track openers

- To an apartment owner: "What if your building cost thirty percent less to operate? Let's find out where that number really is for you. Start with your utility bills in one view and go from there."
- To a high-rise / HOA contact: "Your building already produces all this data. We make it visible in one place, and then we make the building smart enough to tell you when something is about to break."
- To a business owner (custom build): "You have heard everyone talk about AI. This is what it looks like applied to your actual business: your systems, one view, your knowledge compounding instead of walking out the door."

## What never enters this pitch

- Named-customer details beyond what is public. Mox may be referenced internally; with prospects, say "a large Austin multifamily operator" until Nick approves a reference.
- Specific dollar-savings guarantees.
- Promises that we replace a named system on a schedule. Connect first; replacement is the customer's later choice.
