# Mission — two lanes reported closing one card. Find out how.

## The observation

Two agents both reported closing `cad-serve-reconcile`. One reported the merge of factory
`#55` and the close; the other reported the card *already closed* and then produced the
full four-cell table anyway.

`ALREADY_CLAIMED` exists to make that impossible, and `CARD_CLOSED` exists to stop a second
claim once a close artifact lands. **Either the queue permitted something it should not
have, or a lane worked without claiming.** Both are findings and they need different fixes.

This is read-only. Audit the record; change nothing.

## The record exists — read it rather than reasoning about it

Every claim, refusal and release is appended to `_queue/log/<seat>.jsonl`. That is the whole
reason it is written. Reconstruct the exact timeline for `cad-serve-reconcile`: every
`claim` GRANTED, every `claim` REFUSED with its codes, every `release` with its reason, and
the timestamps.

**Exclude the verification events.** `_queue/log/property.jsonl` carries a
`VERIFICATION_MARKER` entry; everything before it was generated by verifying the queue by
violation at setup and is not a real claim. A control verified by violation produces events
indistinguishable from the ones being counted, which is why the marker is there.

## Four mechanisms. Name the one that happened and why you rejected the others.

1. **Release then reclaim.** Lane A claimed, worked, released, and lane B then legitimately
   claimed a card that was open again. The queue behaved correctly and the question becomes
   whether `release` should be permitted before a close artifact exists.
2. **Lease expiry.** The lease TTL is 90 minutes. If lane A's claim expired mid-work the
   card became reclaimable while lane A was still working it, and `ALREADY_CLAIMED` stopped
   blocking exactly when it was most needed.
3. **Worked without claiming.** The bypass named in `QUEUE-INIT` as *honestly advisory* —
   nothing forces a seat to run the CLI. If that is what happened, the bypass has now
   occurred for real rather than in theory.
4. **One lane, reported twice.** The operator relayed two statuses that describe the same
   lane. Cheap to check and it would mean there is no defect at all.

**Do not stop at the first plausible explanation.** State the mechanism you believe, then
state a second that would produce the same log and why you rejected it. That is the
documented recurring error in this operation.

## Then answer the design question the incident raises

**Should `release` refuse when the card's close artifact does not exist?**

Today `release` removes the claim and frees the store token, and nothing checks whether the
work finished. A seat that releases without closing returns the card to the board in a state
indistinguishable from untouched — so a second seat redoes work that is already done, and
two closes could disagree about the same card.

The counter-argument is real: a seat must be able to abandon a card it cannot complete, and
a `release` that refuses without a close would strand the store token until the lease
expires. **Recommend a shape.** An abandon that is recorded as an abandon rather than
prohibited is one option; a required reason is another; refusing outright is a third and
probably wrong.

Consider also whether a lease should be **extendable** by the holder, since mechanism 2 is
only dangerous because a long card silently loses its claim.

## What this is not

**This is not about whether the duplicated work was wasteful.** The second lane's table is
the better artifact and it is the one being used. A seat continuing work it has context for
is not misconduct, and the finding here is about what the control did, not about anyone's
judgement.

**Do not treat "the bypass exists" as a reason to close it.** `QUEUE-INIT` already recorded
it as honestly advisory, and closing it would turn the queue into a dispatcher, which the
L3 fleet ruling forbids. If mechanism 3 is what happened, the useful output is what it cost
this time and what would make claiming the path of least resistance rather than an
obligation.
