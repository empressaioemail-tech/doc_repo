# AF1 league pitch

## The concept

Pitch Arena Football One directly: AF1 becomes the first crypto-native professional league. Two tiers: an AF1 league token at the top (league-wide fan network, cross-team collectibility, league sponsor inventory) and a franchise template every team runs (team token, fan accounts, verified athletes, commerce, venue-ready records). AF1 commissions the build; Empressa builds and operates; the ATX Bulls are presented as the template already applied. Framed so it reads as the league's model, applied first in Austin.

## The strategic logic (internal; not in the pitch materials)

1. **The 3.1(a) inversion.** The Purchase Agreement's prohibition on publicly traded team ownership is a wall for any single team and a wedge at league level: the League controls the very agreements that create the wall. Only AF1 can sanction a tradable token model across its franchises. What blocks the bottom-up path is precisely what makes the top-down pitch necessary and valuable to them.
2. **Structural fit.** AF1 operates more centrally than major leagues (league membership structure, league revenue share, central payroll). A league-native token layer maps onto economics the league already controls, making the league the natural issuer and governor.
3. **Timing.** The March 2026 joint guidance opened US fan tokens; no US league owns the category. First-mover press value is real for a challenger league.
4. **The Bulls flywheel.** The team engagement proves the template cheaply; the league deal turns one build into a rollout across every franchise, paid for by the league. Each strengthens the other: Cody's team gets league-sanctioned status for its program; the league gets a proven artifact instead of a proposal.

## CORRECTED ARCHITECTURE (Nick, 2026-08-13, supersedes the fan-token-centric framing in the artifacts above)

The tradable instrument is NOT the fan layer. Three layers:

1. **Fan layer (per team, unchanged):** the consumptive platform exactly as built for the Bulls (accounts, founding pass, memberships, drops, twins). Stays non-tradable, stays the demand engine. This is the business the token points at.
2. **Business/franchise token layer (AF1-issued, tradable):** the league issues the tradable capital instrument, linked to the franchise brand and/or territory (assets the LEAGUE owns or licenses, not membership interests in team LLCs, which 3.1(a) walls off). Candidate structure: one master AF1 token with per-franchise sub-tokens. This is securities-side BY DESIGN, structured by Nick's tokenomics team plus counsel; it is the capital-formation layer, not merchandise.
3. **RWA enhancer:** tradable liquidity provides capital for the LEAGUE to own stadiums, with each team responsible for assembling its project (siting, feasibility, entitlement, on our stack). The venue links to the franchise sub-token as its real-asset enhancer, with the twin as continuous verified disclosure. This inverts the minor-league norm (teams leasing municipal arenas) into an asset-owning league.

Execution note: Nick has a team forming with a tokenomics expert to execute the token design. Our lane: the fan layer, the referent/verification layer (twins, smart files disclosure, linkage attestations), and the rails (fan graph, payments, splits, metering). The tokenomics team's lane: supply, liquidity architecture, listings, treasury, legal wrapper. The pitch artifacts (slide, brief, financial model) reflect the earlier fan-token-centric frame and need re-cutting once the token design settles.
