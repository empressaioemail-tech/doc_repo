# The fan platform: one account, three products, a twin ecosystem

## The organizing idea

The franchise runs as a twin ecosystem. Players carry verified twins (the A1 program). Fans carry accounts that are fan twins in everything but name: a record of themselves as a fan that accrues history, status, and property. The venue, when it exists, is born a twin. Every revenue product on this platform is an edge between twins: a fan's access to a player's verified record, a fan's claim on a numbered object, a sponsor's access to the registered fan graph. The pass is not the product. The account is the product; the pass is its founding artifact.

The word "token" retires. The instrument is the pass; the container is the account; the language stays consumptive everywhere.

## The fan journey

| Stage | When | What happens in the account | What money changes hands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | now | Free account creation; a dated "Day One era" marker on every account created before first kickoff; the fan graph registers source and consent | none; the free claim IS the capture |
| Found | now to season start | Founding pass purchase (numbered, capped); seat-priority deposits; first merch drops, member-first | pass sales, deposits, merch |
| Meet the team | preseason (late Aug 2026 onward) | A1 baseline testing becomes content: meet the roster through verified numbers, testing-day video, pick-your-players follows | membership upsell, merch, sponsor inventory |
| Season | Mar 2027 onward | Gameday loop: tickets in the account, attendance recorded on the fan twin (streaks, badges), weekly twin content, trivial-matter votes, moment drops after wins | tickets (existing), merch attach, premium content, drops |
| Offseason | post-season | Year-in-review fan card (your season, rendered); renewal window; roster-move content | annual membership renewal, renewal-window drops |

The retention mechanic is the account itself: a fan's record (member number, day-one marker, attendance streak, votes cast, objects owned) is accumulating property that resets to zero anywhere else. Leaving costs history. That is the same moat logic as the rest of the portfolio, pointed at fandom.

## The three products inside the account

**1. The pass and membership (status and access).** Two instruments, deliberately different shapes: the **Founding series** is one-time, numbered, capped, permanent (the collectible; the "Founding Bull" status never renews because it never expires) and the **annual membership** is the recurring layer (access tier for the current season: member pricing, content access, priority windows, votes). Founding holders get the first year of membership included and their status survives whether or not they renew. This split is what makes revenue consistent rather than spiky: the founding drop is launch capital; the membership is the annuity.

**2. Player twin access (the content engine and the differentiator).** Three access rings on every player twin: public sees the verified basics (the card: position, verified height and weight, headline A1 numbers with their measured-how badges); members see depth (full A1 results, testing-day video, progression across the three administrations, injury-return states told honestly); premium or per-player followers see the close view (player-produced content anchored to the twin, mic'd testing days, watch-my-season). The A1 protocol is what makes any of this sellable: every number carries evidence, and "the first team whose fans see the receipts" is a claim no incumbent can copy. Fans follow specific players; a follow is a watch on a twin, and it is also a signal the sponsorship layer can price.

**3. Commerce (merch and operated sales).** We operate the sales rail: one account, one cart, one checkout across pass, membership, merch, and (when the ticketing relationship allows) tickets. Merch integrates with the twin ecosystem instead of sitting beside it: member-first and member-priced drops; numbered physical items whose ownership is recorded on the fan's account (the jersey is also an entry on your twin); player-linked items that carry the player's verified card with them and route the player's cut automatically. Drop cadence gives the calendar its commerce moments: founding drop, roster-reveal drop, opening-night drop, playoff drops.

## Revenue streams above tickets (the franchise P and L view)

1. Founding series (one-time, high margin, launch window).
2. Annual membership (recurring; the base layer this whole design exists to create).
3. Merch through the operated rail (member conversion should beat the current standalone store meaningfully; the account is a permission asset).
4. Premium twin content (season-long upsell and per-player follows, revenue-shared with players).
5. Sponsorship on the digital surfaces (testing day presented-by, drop sponsorships, segment-targeted offers to the registered graph). This line is underrated: a verified, consented, segmented fan graph is exactly what local sponsors cannot get from arena signage, and it scales with list size, not attendance.
6. Later and separate doors: the revenue-share note (offering flow, counsel-gated, never sharing a surface with any of the above) and stadium-era products.

The honest magnitude statement: at a 2,000-contact list this is tens of thousands in the founding window; the design's job is that every stream compounds with list growth toward opening night and across seasons, and that the machinery (account, rail, twin content) is built once. Consistency comes from membership renewals plus a drop calendar plus season content cadence; lucrative comes from margin (digital products at near-zero marginal cost, merch at member conversion, sponsorship at graph scale).

## How players make money with their twin (rough model, and the positioning it buys)

Context that makes this matter: AF1 players are paid through league-central payroll at league-set levels (per the Purchase Agreement's assessment structure). Supplemental income is a real recruitment lever at this tier.

1. **Content revenue share.** Premium follows and player-anchored content pay the player a defined share automatically through the account rail. The player's twin is the anchor; the fan's follow is the subscription.
2. **Per-reference royalties.** When a player's verified data is accessed by external parties (scouts, media, other teams' analysts), the access is metered and the player accrues a per-reference royalty. This is the obligation-meter pattern the platform already runs elsewhere, pointed at athletes.
3. **Verified collectibles.** Player cards and moment drops carrying A1-verified numbers, with an automatic player cut on primary sales. (Secondary markets stay off until the transferability posture changes program-wide.)
4. **Marketplace services.** Appearances, shoutouts, camps, lessons, booked and paid through the account with the player's twin as the storefront and the rail taking a platform fee.
5. **Sponsor matching on verified claims.** A gate-timed fastest-man-in-the-league number is a sellable, evidenced marketing claim; the platform brokers player-sponsor matches on verified data and takes a fee, with the athlete's consent key controlling every disclosure.
6. **The portable record.** The twin travels with the athlete after the Bulls (tenant-shared; the athlete owns their side). A verified multi-season record is career capital for tryouts upward. This costs the franchise nothing and is worth the most in the locker room.

**The positioning consequence for the A1 program:** testing is not surveillance and not just sports science; it is the athlete's earning asset being built, with consent, evidence, and a payout rail. That is the sentence that makes intake consent easy, makes the players allies of the program, and makes the Bulls' recruiting pitch different from every other team at this level: come here and your performance becomes verified, visible, and monetizable.

**The positioning consequence for the pass:** the pass is the fan-side key to the twin economy: founding status plus the right to see and follow what the A1 program produces. It is priced and marketed as membership in the first fully-twinned franchise, never as an instrument.

## Sequencing note inside workstream 1

Account and capture first (it is the substrate for everything, including the merch rail); founding drop plus deposits second (the money moment against the current wave); A1 content layer third (preseason clock supplies it); membership annuity and player payout rails harden through the season. ## Open design questions

2. Ticketing integration reality: whether tickets can live in the account year one or link out (venue and ticketing vendor unknown until the venue answer exists).
3. Player payout mechanics: paid as marketing income through the team, or direct platform payouts to athletes (touches tax and league assessment questions; counsel and accountant input needed before promises to players).
4. League posture on player-data commercialization (doc 06 item, still open) and on the operated merch rail versus any league-wide merch arrangements (check the unseen Operating Agreement).
5. How much of this platform is Bulls-branded surface versus the first deployment of a reusable franchise fan-platform product (portfolio question, not a Cody question; parked).
