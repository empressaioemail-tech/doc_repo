# Unified Data and AI for Multifamily Operations
**Prepared for Mox**

## What you get

Mox already runs on dozens of systems. Yardi, your CRM, accounting and payroll, marketing tools, construction-side software, and everything in between. Each one captures a piece of your business. None of them talk to each other. Institutional knowledge evaporates between them every day.

What we build is the layer underneath — connective tissue that captures what every system produces, unifies it into a single structured data layer, and makes it travel across all your communities and all three lines of business.

What you get is institutional knowledge that compounds in value every month, instead of evaporating with every staff transition, software change, or vendor swap.

## How it works

**Connect first, replace later — if ever.** We integrate underneath your existing stack. No rip and replace, no rug pull, no two-year migration before anything starts working. Your systems stay in place. Your workflows keep running. Your teams keep using the tools they already know. Over time, as the intelligence layer matures, you decide which legacy systems remain sources of truth and which become optional or get replaced with custom-tailored solutions. The sequencing is your decision, on your timeline.

**Capture once, use everywhere.** What your team figures out at one property surfaces to the team facing the same situation at another. Institutional knowledge stops evaporating with turnover, software changes, or vendor transitions.

**You own everything.** Today your institutional knowledge is functionally locked inside the platforms that hold it — and you already know what migrating off Yardi would look like. Under this layer, you own your data on day one. If you ever decide to move on, you walk out with everything: your decisions, your operating history, your competitive intelligence. The data leaves with you, not with the vendor.

**One dashboard, many lenses.** The MOX dashboard becomes the surface your teams live in. Yardi and your other systems remain sources of truth as long as you want them to be — they become tabs on the dashboard rather than your default window. Role-based access keeps each team in its own lane (property accountants, site managers, acquisitions analysts, construction supers, brokers, residents), enforced at the data layer, not at the screen.

## One layer, three lines of business

The same data layer serves Manage, Own, and Build — and the equal weight is the point. Today an asset's information lives in fragments: acquisitions data in one tool, construction scope in another, lease-up in a third, monthly close in a fourth, disposition reconstructed from scratch every time a buyer asks. Under the intelligence layer, every line of business feeds the next.

### Manage — the operating engine

The day-to-day operating layer your teams already work in, made faster as the team's collective experience compounds across the portfolio.

- **Monthly close is faster and self-explaining.** Variance commentary drafts itself from prior months' patterns. Vendor invoices arrive pre-coded. Property accountants review exceptions instead of writing the same explanation 45 times.
- **Site teams learn from each other automatically.** What worked at one property surfaces to the team facing the same situation elsewhere. Inspections, work orders, vendor performance, renewal patterns all compound as institutional knowledge.
- **Resident lifecycle as structured data.** Tour, application, lease, move-in, renewal, move-out — all visible, all queryable across the portfolio.

### Own — the capital side

The acquisitions, asset management, and disposition layer where every decision is made against the operating reality of every prior asset under Mox management.

- **Acquisitions underwriting against operating reality.** The pro forma references real line-item operating expenses from your existing portfolio. The underwrite reflects what your team will actually deliver under Mox management, not just what the seller's broker promises.
- **Continuous performance against the underwrite.** Asset management sees actuals versus original underwrite in real time, not at quarterly close. Recap decisions reference the full operating history without anyone reconstructing it.
- **Disposition without reconstruction.** When you sell or refinance, the buyer or lender receives a complete, structured operating record. The lift you delivered under Mox management is documented in the data, not pieced together in a sales pitch.

### Build — the construction arm

Rehab and build economics where multi-jurisdiction code intelligence, pre-submission plan review, and a queryable history of every prior job replace the current deal-by-deal re-discovery.

- **Multi-state, multi-jurisdiction code intelligence.** Your construction arm relearns a different regulatory regime for every state and every market you operate in — setbacks, parking minimums, accessibility, energy codes, multifamily-specific overlays. We load every relevant state and jurisdiction's code into one queryable layer your team works from.
- **Pre-submission plan review.** Every resubmission cycle is three to six weeks of carrying cost. The intelligence layer runs the same review the authority-having-jurisdiction will run, catches what they would catch, and lets you submit clean the first time.
- **Scope and materials library.** Every rehab and build produces structured data on scope, materials, costs, performance, and warranty outcomes — queryable by scope type, jurisdiction, and vintage. The next bid leverages every prior job instead of starting over.
- **Subcontractor and cost intelligence.** Per-sub reliability and per-line-item cost data accumulated across every prior job. Today this lives in someone's head. Tomorrow it's data the next bid and the next change order draw on automatically.

## Visualization — the surface your operators and partners actually see

Data is half the asset; what you can see is the other half. The intelligence layer ships with visualization built in.

- **Operating dashboards** that show portfolio-wide performance with drill-through to a single unit's history, role-scoped automatically.
- **Construction scope visualization** so a site walk produces marked-up plans, photos, and punch lists that flow back to the same property record as structured data.
- **Before-and-after rendering for value-add scopes** — what the unit looks like today, what it looks like after the scope, what the projected rent lift is. Useful internally for scoping, useful externally when communicating the play.
- **Capital-partner views** — the same operating record presented at the depth and format each audience needs, from a quarterly update to a refi package to a buyer's due-diligence room.
- **Resident-facing surfaces** that share the same data layer, so what a resident sees and what your team sees never drift.

## How this gets built

Concrete enough to commit to, flexible enough to adjust as we go. Every phase ships something each department can actually touch — not internal infrastructure work hidden until a big-bang reveal.

**Discovery (4–6 weeks).** A working session series across your leadership. We map the org, identify the friction each team feels day-to-day, and select the first wedges based on where the before-and-after is cleanest and your teams are ready to engage. Output: a delivery plan with named first-90-day wins and a year-one roadmap.

*What your team sees:* leadership conversations, plus early mockups of what each department's working surface will look like — shown back to the people who'll actually use them so we confirm we heard you right.

**Months 1–3.** Foundation. Unified data layer in place underneath your existing systems. MOX dashboard live. First wedge shipping — most likely monthly close and variance, given the visibility and the cleanness of the metric.

*What your team sees:* the accounting team gets a sandbox version of their close workflow with pre-coded invoices and pre-drafted variance commentary. They review, edit, reject — we learn from what they change. By the end of the quarter the sandbox is the workflow.

**Months 3–6.** First Build-side capability live — most likely multi-jurisdiction code intelligence and pre-submission plan review, given the carrying-cost math.

*What your team sees:* every department gets its own sandbox — a sample site, real-but-isolated data, full permission to play, find bugs, push back, and tell us what works and what doesn't. It's chaotic on purpose. That's how we learn how you actually work, so the production surface fits your team instead of the other way around.

**Months 6–12.** Parallel build-out across Manage, Own, and Build. Capabilities sequence to where you and your teams want the next visible win, not to an arbitrary roadmap.

*What your team sees:* the sandboxes graduate into the daily workflow, refined against the feedback you gave us in months 3–6. By the end of year one the intelligence layer is doing meaningful work in every line of business — not as something IT bolted on, but as something each team helped build.

**Year 2 and beyond.** The flywheel takes over. Every disposed asset's history becomes a comparable for the next acquisition. Every completed renovation's actual cost and timeline informs the next construction underwrite. Every lease-up's velocity informs the next pro forma. Every adjudicated code interpretation in any of your markets — Texas or beyond — becomes precedent the next deal in that market draws on. The flywheel never reverses.

## What you own at the end

The institutional knowledge produced by your team every month is captured, structured, and compounding. You own all of it. You control where it lives. You are not locked in to us — if you ever decide to move on, you keep everything in a format that still works in any tool you choose.

You also get out of the AI and software shopping cycle. Today, evaluating, piloting, implementing, and eventually replacing each new vertical tool — leasing AI, maintenance triage, renewal pricing, AP automation — is a recurring six-figure overhead in direct cost and in productivity lost every time the team has to learn another system. The intelligence layer becomes the durable foundation underneath. Future tools plug in on top of it or come out without you losing the data, the institutional knowledge, or the dollars you spent capturing them.

The intelligence layer becomes the durable asset of the company, sitting above any individual software vendor, including ours.
