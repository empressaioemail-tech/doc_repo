# Category white space — one-pager brief (channel enablement)

The page that turns a Vertosoft or Forrest rep from listing the product into championing it. It answers the question every rep silently asks: why does nothing else on my shelf do this, and why should I believe that will last? It is an inside page: it names market structure and competitors descriptively, which buyer-facing pages never do.

## Audience and job

The channel rep, before their first city conversation. Job: believe the category is real and uncontested, be able to verify that belief independently, and know exactly which sentences they are allowed to repeat to a buyer (the claim column) versus which stay in the room (the terrain).

## Distribution rule

Channel enablement only. Never attached to a city proposal, never left behind at a demo, never published on the website. Mark the footer accordingly.

## Page anatomy and copy

### Header

SmartCity OS mark, "CHANNEL BRIEFING" instead of "PRODUCT ONE-PAGER". No category chip.

### Hero

**Headline:** Why There Is Nothing Else Like This on Your Shelf

**Subhead:** The data about every place in America exists and does not function. The vendors around that problem each hold one piece of it. Nobody sells the fix. Here is the shape of the gap, and the four claims no competitor can make.

### The terrain band: four cards

**Card 1: The data holders.** Over three thousand counties and municipalities hold the data: published as PDF, fragmented across every county line, siloed inside each government, stale with no freshness signal, asserted with no provenance. Forty-five years of national-standardization calls, no mandate, every federal attempt at a national layer dead.

**Card 2: The aggregators.** National parcel coverage, but what they sell is flat rows: geometry plus attributes. No reasoning, no citation chain, no currency checking. Their coverage is achieved by scraping and digitizing whatever exists, so their pipeline inherits every failure above and papers over it.

**Card 3: The workflow incumbents.** Permit systems, work-order systems, assessment hosting. Each is a system of transaction for one silo. None is a system of record about the place itself, and some contractually restrict a government from publishing its own data.

**Card 4: The scale players.** Big-government platforms priced for big government, skipping the thousands of small and mid-size cities entirely.

### The gap band (dark accent)

**Header:** The layer nobody sells.

**Body:** The reconciled, cited, current, access-controlled record of a place, with reasoning on top of it. Where the flood zone, the zoning, the parcel, the asset, and the decision about them are one connected record that carries where each fact came from, how current it is, and who may see it. That layer is the product, delivered to cities as Dashboards, Plan Review, and Asset Management on one foundation.

### The evidence band (research-backed, added 2026-08-07)

A row of three or four fact boxes between the terrain and the table. Every figure here is third-party, dated, and independently checkable, which is the entire point of this page. Sources are the filed research notes in `../whitespace_package/` (R11, R15, R16, R20) and the 2026-07-28 storefront scan; do not add figures beyond these.

**Fact box 1: The shelf is empty.** 35 listings on this storefront as of July 2026. None in jurisdictional intelligence, property reasoning, or plan review. The nearest adjacency is a traffic dashboard.

**Fact box 2: The machines are not served.** Roughly 39,000 local governments; about 10,000 of 20,000 permitting jurisdictions do not publish permits online at all, and no US jurisdiction serves its zoning rules through a public API. A federal pilot measured AI accuracy on public data at 0 to 2 percent without a machine interface, and about 95 percent with one.

**Fact box 3: The fix never came from above.** Six federal attempts at a national property-data layer since 1980, none implemented. The federal government now licenses commercial parcel data for its own use.

**Fact box 4 (optional): The cost is measured.** The only state that mandates measurement prices a public records request at roughly $265 and 3.5 staff hours, and parcel and permit records are among the two most-requested categories. Cities that publish the high-demand data saw request volume drop 30 to 43 percent in the one large study.

### The conviction table

Render as a two-column table, the centerpiece of the page. Title: **Documented problem, and the claim only we make.**

| The documented failure of government data | What we say, and no one else can |
|---|---|
| Facts asserted with no lineage, no confidence, no as-of date | Every fact carries its source, its date, and a confidence level |
| Data published once, decaying with no freshness signal | Currency is checked, not assumed |
| Silos inside one government; departments that do not agree on the same parcel | One record underneath, not four systems stitched |
| Access moving backward: paywalls, vendor portals, lock-in on the government's own data | It is yours and it stays |

**Caption under the table:** The left column is documented in third-party literature and government audits, not asserted by us. Verify it independently; that is the point. (Sourced notes with per-claim citations are on file; per-row grounding is recorded in `../whitespace_package/01_category_whitespace_and_conviction_map.md`.)

### The rep guidance band

**Header:** What you repeat to a city, and what stays in this room.

**Body:** The right column is yours to say, in the product wording on each product one-pager. The left column shapes how you listen: when a city manager describes departments that disagree or records that left with a retirement, you are hearing these failures firsthand. The terrain on this page stays here: never name a competitor to a buyer, never sell the gap. Sell the relief.

### Proof line

Live with the City of Bastrop, Texas. City leadership runs the city on it day to day.

### Footer

CHANNEL BRIEFING. Internal to the partner sales team. Not for distribution to customers.

## Claims discipline for this page

The terrain cards describe market structure descriptively and may name vendor categories, but no named-competitor sentence on this page may be phrased as attack copy a rep could lift into a buyer conversation. Figures are permitted on this page ONLY in the evidence band above, only as written there, and only because each is third-party, dated, and sourced in the filed research notes; the aggregator-claimed coverage triangulations (the "90% of counties" class of number) remain barred everywhere. No cycle-time, savings, or ROI figure about OUR product exists or may be invented. Everything on the never-say list in `00_onepager_system.md` applies; "digital twin" and the architecture vocabulary stay out even here, because a rep repeats what they read.
