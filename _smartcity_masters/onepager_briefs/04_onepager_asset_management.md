# Asset Management — one-pager brief

The category the city manager has no vendor to compare to. The draft's hero instinct was right (record, then live condition, then view); what it lacked was the pain beat, the connectedness claim, and the delivery framing. This is also the page where the "which properties lose service" line belongs, stated as a property of the system rather than as a smart-report feature.

## Audience and job

Sell the city manager; serve public works. The page speaks to the manager: the durable record and the single connected picture. It never pitches work-order features, which would invite a losing comparison to the public works incumbents, and it never asks the city to replace what public works already runs.

## Page anatomy and copy

### Hero

**Headline:** A Record of Your City You Own and Can Prove

**Subhead:** Every physical thing your city is responsible for, in one place. Water mains to sidewalks to vehicles: where each one is, what condition it is in, what has been done to it, and who changed it and when. From the paperwork, to its live condition, to a visual view when you need one.

### Pain strip

Today an asset lives in a utility layer, a work-order system, a spreadsheet, or a set of as-built drawings in a filing cabinet, and none of them know anything about each other, or about the properties, roads, and flood zones around them. When the person who built that knowledge retires, it leaves with them.

### What it does: four cards

**Card 1: One connected picture, not another silo.** Your assets sit alongside your properties, roads, terrain, and flood data, not in a system that knows nothing about any of it. "Which properties lose water if this main fails, and are any of them in the flood zone" is a query, not a project.

**Card 2: A record you can prove.** Every asset carries where it came from, when it was established, and every change since. Nothing is silently overwritten. Years from now, the record still holds up.

**Card 3: You decide who sees what.** Public assets can be public. The sewer network can be visible only to public works and the contractors they authorize. A sensitive facility can be locked to a named few. That control is built into the record itself.

**Card 4: Live condition, kept.** Sensor and telemetry readings attach to the asset they measure, and every reading becomes part of that asset's permanent record. See how an asset has actually behaved over time, not just what it reads right now.

### Delivery band

**Header:** Built with you, around what you already have.

**Body:** No two cities have the same assets or the same starting point. We connect what public works already runs and build out from there. What we build depends on your assets and your priorities; what does not change is the structure underneath.

### Proof band

Live with the City of Bastrop, Texas, where the connected base already holds 74,729 parcels, the full road network, and the zoning, flood, and terrain layers alongside them.

The counts are approved ONLY as the connected public-record base. Never present them as city-owned assets under management; there are none yet, and no count of assets under management may appear anywhere.

### Footer

**Grow-into line:** Grow into it when you're ready, deepening the same record Dashboards already runs on.

**CTA:** Schedule a demonstration to see Asset Management on your city's data.

## Claims discipline for this page

Not approved, do not print: any count of city-owned assets under management. Any 3D delivery commitment or date (a "visual view" is as far as the page goes). Tokenization, on-chain, or chain-bridge claims of any kind. GovTitle, ever. Any named vendor feed as connected at a city that has not granted it. Any named-competitor comparison, including the public works incumbents. Everything on the never-say list in `00_onepager_system.md`.
