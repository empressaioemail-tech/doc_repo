# Dashboards — one-pager brief

The entry product. This page carries the most weight of the four: it is what a city buys first, and it must read as a complete system with a defined outcome, never as a starter tier.

## Audience and job

City manager. Job: see their own daily frustration in the pain strip, understand the lens model in plain terms, and register the two claims no competitor can make: one record underneath, and what comes in is kept.

## Page anatomy and copy

### Hero

**Headline:** One Pane of Glass to Run Your City

**Subhead:** And the right view for everyone who runs a piece of it. The city manager sees across everything. Development services sees the pipeline. Finance sees the money. Residents see what is theirs. Every view drawn from the same record, so the numbers agree and the map is the same map.

This replaces the draft's "One View, Total Clarity," which is generic vendor language. The approved one-liner above is the differentiated form.

### Pain strip

Six departments and a dozen systems that do not talk. Staff answering the same property question over and over. Nobody able to see the whole city at once. Institutional knowledge leaving with every retirement.

### What it does: four cards

**Card 1: One view of the whole city, and the right view for each department.** Not another login. Not another system that disagrees with the last one.

**Card 2: Everyone sees what is theirs, and only what is theirs.** Who can see what is built into the record itself, not managed screen by screen. You decide who sees what.

**Card 3: What comes in is kept.** When a system connects, what it measures becomes part of your city's permanent record, so you can see how something has actually behaved over time, not just what it reads right now.

**Card 4: Departments that come later inherit everything already there.** The structure is established once. Each department is a view onto it. Adding one does not mean buying and securing another system.

Cut from the draft: the Morning Briefing card (not an approved claim) and the Natural-Language Queries card as written. If a fourth card slot needs an assistant line instead of the expansion card, the only approved form is: "An assistant that follows you through the system and can actually answer, because everything underneath it was built to be read." Nothing beyond that sentence.

### One-record band

**Header:** One record underneath, not four systems stitched.

**Body:** Every vendor shows role-based views. Ours agree because they are the same record. That is the difference between watching your city and knowing it.

### The refusal band (homed here 2026-08-07 — this is its page)

The front door's closing band became the results band in the locked render, so the refusal lives on this page and only this page. It is not optional here.

**Header:** We will not sell you the shallow version.

**Body:** We do not sell a screen that just shows your existing systems in a nicer layout. If that is what you want, you already have it. When a system connects to us, what it measures becomes a permanent part of your city's record.

A refusal is credible in a market full of claims. If the page has room for one more band, this is the one.

### Proof band

Live with the City of Bastrop, Texas. City leadership runs the city on it day to day. Usable from a phone.

### Footer

**Grow-into line:** A complete, deployable system on day one. Deployment establishes the foundation your city grows from: review against your own code, a record of your own assets.

**CTA:** Schedule a demonstration to see Dashboards on your city's data.

## Claims discipline for this page

Not approved, do not print: citizen payment processing. Any named vendor feed as connected at any city that has not granted it. Any count of departments live at a customer. Morning briefing. Any 3D or sensor-hardware claim (that altitude belongs to Asset Management, within its own limits). Dashboards as phase one, in any wording.
