# SmartCity one-pagers — the system

Read this first, then the brief for the artifact you are producing. The briefs carry placement-ready copy. Your job is layout, hierarchy, and polish. Do not invent copy, do not summarize the masters yourself, and do not import language from any other product line.

## What these artifacts are for

Channel collateral. The primary consumer is a channel partner (Vertosoft) whose reps will repeat what the page says, word for word, to city buyers. A claim on these pages travels farther than we can see it, so every sentence is either from an approved claims register or a description of the buyer's own world that makes no claim about us.

The buyer is the city manager of a small-to-medium municipality. Not a technologist. The reps sell use-case-first: the problem solved, never the technology.

## The pitch structure every page follows

From the positioning framework (`../35_smartcity_positioning_framework.md`). Three beats, in order, never reordered:

1. **Pain first.** The page opens with the problem as the buyer experiences it. Disconnected systems, money out the door, knowledge walking out with staff. This requires no belief in anything we claim, which is why it opens.
2. **What you get.** The mechanism and the capability, in the buyer's language.
3. **Their city, concretely.** Their departments, their code, their assets, their record. The close makes it about them, not us.

The 2026-08 drafts skipped beat one entirely. Every page opened with product structure. That is the single biggest correction in this generation.

## Page anatomy

Seven zones, top to bottom. The briefs give copy per zone.

| Zone | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Header | SmartCity OS mark, "PRODUCT ONE-PAGER", category chip |
| Hero | Headline plus subhead. Relief stated over the pain |
| Pain strip | One or two sentences naming the buyer's problem. Beat one. New in this generation |
| What it does | Three cards minimum, four where the brief provides one. The drafts ran two and left the page half empty |
| One-record band | The shared-foundation claim, replacing the "SHARED WITH X" labels |
| Proof band | The Bastrop claim, only on pages where the brief includes it |
| Footer | Grow-into line plus the demonstration CTA |

Fill the page. The Plan Review and Asset Management drafts had large empty regions because only two cards were placed. Every brief in this folder carries at least three cards plus band copy; use all of it.

## Copy rules

**Verbatim or nothing.** Headlines, cards, and bands come from the artifact brief. You may tighten for fit (drop a clause to fit a card) but never paraphrase into new claims, and never "improve" a line by adding a number, an outcome, or an adjective the brief does not carry.

**Figures rule.** Numbers appear on a page only where a brief explicitly provides them, with their attribution, exactly as written. Never add a statistic, a percentage, or a dollar figure on your own, however well it seems to fit — the approved figures are third-party problem-scale facts with citations on file, and anything else is either our unmeasured outcome (barred) or an unverified aggregator claim (barred).

**The never-say list, absolute, on every page:** digital twin, RWA, tokenization, on-chain, blockchain, atom, node, graph, substrate, storage layer, IPFS, content-addressed, distributed storage. Codex, GovTitle, Bluebeam, CitizenConnect. No competitor named. No cycle-time, savings, percentage, or ROI figure of any kind. No claim that the system approves, permits, or certifies. No verbatim body text from any licensed building code, in copy or in screenshots. No 3D promise or date. No citizen payment processing. Never describe Dashboards as phase one. Never name or diagram the foundation.

**No cross-line borrowing.** Smart Site professional collateral (realtors, architects, developers, investors) and SmartCity city collateral never share language. The draft overview page carried "the thing a realtor shares with a client, an architect hands to a reviewer, a developer runs before they buy" on a city page. Wrong audience, wrong line. City pages describe smart reports in city terms only (see the overview brief).

**The foundation gets one sentence, once, on the overview page only:** "The way we capture and process data is the foundation for everything else to get built." It is never named, never diagrammed, never given a card.

## Corrections to the 2026-08 draft generation

Recorded so the same mistakes are not remade.

1. No pain beat anywhere. Every page now opens with one (hero subhead or pain strip).
2. Pages half empty. Two cards where the masters supply three or four leads. Use the full card set in each brief.
3. Dashboards carried a "Morning Briefing" card. Not in any approved claims register. Cut until approved by the operator.
4. Dashboards carried a "Natural-Language Queries" card claiming plain-language answers from the current record. The approved form is the assistant line in the Dashboards brief; use it exactly, or cut the card.
5. The Smart Report band used Smart Site professional framing and enumerated layers freely. City pages use the city framing and name only the two live reports: the Smart Site X-ray and the flood and drainage study. Never a longer menu.
6. "SHARED WITH ASSET MANAGEMENT" / "SHARED WITH PLAN REVIEW" labels. Confusing and backwards. The point is one record under everything; use the one-record band copy in each brief.
7. No proof anywhere. "Live with the City of Bastrop, Texas" is approved and is the strongest thing these pages can say. It appears on Dashboards, Asset Management, and the overview. It does NOT appear on Plan Review (production state there is unverified; that page's proof is the mechanism itself).
8. Dashboards hero "One View, Total Clarity" is generic vendor language. The approved one-liner is stronger; the brief carries it.

## What was right in the drafts, keep it

The visual system itself: dark hero, category chip, white card grid, dark accent band, full-width CTA footer. The "Grow into it when you're ready" footer line and its framing. The Asset Management hero's record-to-live-condition-to-view ordering. The Plan Review CTA "on your city's code." The refusal to call anything phase one.
