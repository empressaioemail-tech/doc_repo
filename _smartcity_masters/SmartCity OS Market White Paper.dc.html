SmartCity OS

One Platform, Not One More Tool

The problem every city feels

A city manager's day runs across fifteen systems that do not talk to each other. Permits live in one. Work orders in another. Fleet, finance, code enforcement, records, each in its own silo, each with its own login, none of them agreeing with each other when a question crosses department lines.

Ask a question that crosses a department boundary and watch what it takes to answer. Staff log into four systems and reconcile the differences by hand. Residents ask the same question and get a different answer depending on who picks up. The council asks how something is going and the honest answer is that it takes a week to find out.

Underneath that, two things are quietly expensive. Money goes out the door for work a city would rather do in-house, because the staff to do it cannot be hired or kept. And institutional knowledge walks out with every retirement, because it was never written anywhere a system could hold it.

This is not a tooling problem you fix by buying one more system. It is a foundation problem, and it is what SmartCity OS is built to fix.

Three systems your city runs

SmartCity OS is three systems. Each is complete and deployable on its own, with its own outcome. A city buys one, runs it, and grows into the others when it is ready. None of them is a phase of something else.

Dashboards

One pane of glass to run your city, and the right view for everyone who runs a piece of it. The city manager sees across everything. Development services sees the pipeline. Finance sees the money. Residents see what is theirs. Every view is drawn from the same record, so the numbers agree and the map is the same map. You decide who sees what.

It is not one screen. It is a view per audience, and the structure is established once. Departments that come later inherit everything already there rather than starting another silo.

Plan Review

Send less of your review out the door. Submittals arrive pre-reviewed against your own adopted code, cited section by section, with what passes, what fails, and what needs a human eye already sorted. Your reviewer accepts, edits, or overrides anything, and their decision is recorded with their reasoning. The comment letter comes out of the same flow.

Your staff spend their time on judgment instead of catching the same errors over and over, which means more of your review handled by the people you already have. How your city has decided things becomes part of your record, so consistency does not depend on who is at the desk that day, and the reasoning is still there years later.

Asset Management

Every physical thing your city is responsible for, in one place, with a record you own and can prove. Water mains, sidewalks, lift stations, streetlights, signals, parks, facilities, and vehicles become a permanent, connected record: where each one is, what condition it is in, what has been done to it, and who changed it and when.

Each asset sits in its real place, connected to the roads, properties, and flood zones around it, so a question about one asset can be answered in the context of everything near it. When a system connects and reports on an asset, every reading becomes part of that asset's permanent record, so you can see how it has actually behaved over time rather than only what it reads right now.

This is built with you, around what you already have. We connect what public works already runs and build out from there.

Smart Files, underneath all three

Everything your city holds, in one place you can actually search. A document lives once and shows up everywhere it belongs, so when someone revises it, it is current everywhere at once, and the previous version is still there when you need it. Every person in a city hall has lost a document, found four versions of it, or updated one copy of five. This is that problem, gone. Your data is yours.

What makes this different

Every vendor in this market will show you role-based views over integrated systems. Four things here are not the same, and they are worth checking us on.

One record underneath, not four systems stitched together

The reason the numbers agree across your departments is not that we reconcile them nightly. It is that there is one underlying record and each view is a window onto it. That is the difference between watching your city and knowing it.

Cited to your own code

Findings point at the actual section your city adopted, not a generic rule, with the reasoning openable and your reviewer's judgment governing. We do not approve, permit, or certify anything. That is your city's act, and it stays that way.

It is yours, and it stays

Your city owns its data. You are not locked to us or to any one host. Nothing is silently overwritten: the current version is everywhere it belongs and what it was before is still retrievable. Years from now, the record still holds up, and who changed it and when is part of the record rather than a log somewhere else.

We will not sell you the shallow version

We do not sell a screen that just shows you your existing systems in a nicer layout. If that is what you want, you already have it. When a system connects to us, what it measures becomes a permanent part of your city's record. There is no cheaper tier that skips that, because that is the part that is worth anything in five years.

How a city starts

A city buys one system with a defined outcome and runs it. That is the whole first conversation, and it is complete on its own.

What deployment also does is establish the foundation, and that is what makes the rest possible later: deepening your own assets, running review against your own code, growing a record you own. Those are things a city grows into after the first system is running, not a bundle to sign up for on day one.

Cities commonly fund this kind of modernization through grant programs around resilience, data redundancy, and modernization, and this fits those narratives. It is worth asking early whether there is a program or a budget cycle this could ride.

Proof

SmartCity OS is live with the City of Bastrop, Texas, where city leadership runs the city on it day to day, with operational systems across departments connected and feeding it. It is usable from a phone.

The one view worth asking to see is the city manager's. It is the only view that cannot exist unless everything underneath is genuinely connected, which makes it the proof that the rest of this is true rather than the flashiest thing we could show you.
