# Send Less of Your Review Out the Door

Submittals arrive pre-reviewed against your city's own adopted code, cited section by section. Your staff spend their time on judgment instead of first-pass error-catching, so more review stays in-house and the queue moves.

Cities pay outside firms to do their plan review, not because they want to, but because plans examiners cannot be hired or kept. And review is the bottleneck everyone feels: cycle times stretch, applicants resubmit, staff answer the same code question over and over, and the council hears about it.

## What it does

**Do more with the staff you have.** Submittals arrive pre-reviewed, so your examiners spend their time on judgment rather than first-pass error-catching, and less of your review has to go to an outside firm.

**Cited to your code, every time.** Findings point at the actual section of the code your city adopted, not a generic rule. Open any finding and see what it rests on.

**Your reviewer's judgment governs.** The system proposes, cites, and remembers; your reviewer accepts, edits, or overrides, and their decision is recorded with their reasoning. And when the system cannot judge something, it says so rather than guessing.

**Your decisions stay yours.** How your city has decided things becomes part of your record, so consistency does not depend on who is at the desk that day, and the reasoning is still there years later.

## Every decision lands on the record of the place

A review decision and the property it concerns live in the same record your dashboards read, so the development services view shows what is in flight, what is stuck, and what was decided, all from one source. The comment letter comes out of the same flow.

Plan review sits alongside the smart reports the platform produces: studies a city would otherwise commission or go without. Two are live today: the smart site X-ray, the full read on a place, and the flood and drainage study, which answers what happens to a property in a heavy rain.
