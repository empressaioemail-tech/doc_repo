# Empressa Land, executive summary

## The idea

A landman today runs a book of leases and title out of spreadsheets, filing cabinets, and trips to the courthouse. The public record that governs every one of those assets, ownership, leases, wells, production, who operates what, lives scattered across county clerks, the Railroad Commission, and a handful of expensive tools built for the majors. There is no single, current, trustworthy picture of it, and the small operator and independent landman are the ones left without one.

Empressa Land builds that picture. It is a complete, always-current model of the public oil and gas record, starting in the Permian, connected together and shown on a map you can read at a glance. On top of that public foundation, a company loads its own private book, its leases, interests, obligations, and title work, and sees its assets in the full context of the field. The public part is shared. The private part stays theirs.

## What it does

It pulls the public record and organizes it into one connected model rather than a pile of separate files, then keeps it current. From that model it answers, in seconds, the questions that take hours of courthouse and Railroad Commission digging today. It does four things that matter most to the people who run land.

Title and ownership. It assembles the chain of title for a tract from the recorded instruments, deeds, mineral reservations, assignments, and releases, into a clean run sheet with the source document behind every entry, and lays out the ownership and the fractional interests. Where the record has gaps or defects, it shows them plainly rather than papering over them. It assembles and reasons over the record; the legal opinion stays with counsel.

Leases and obligations. It tracks every lease and every obligation that keeps a lease alive, delay rentals, shut-in payments, expirations, and the rest, so a lease is never lost to a missed deadline. This is the difference between keeping an asset and forfeiting it.

The complete field picture. It shows the whole record as a map with layers you turn on and off: leases, wells, laterals, pipelines, disposal wells, fields, and a time slider that lets you watch operators consolidate and production decline across the years. The surface and the subsurface in one view.

The private book. A company loads its own leases, interests, economics, and even seismic, drawn privately over the public base and visible only to them. Their data never mixes into anyone else's.

## Who it is for

Independent landmen, wildcatters, and small operators first, the people running a book by hand today. The platform is built so a large operator can load a book of tens of thousands of leases later, but the independent is who it is built for first. Over time, as books come onto the platform, it becomes the place assets are not just managed but valued and sold, and where the capital behind a deal can move on facts it can trust.

## What makes it different

It is one connected model, not a stack of separate tools that do not talk to each other. It is always current, drawn from the source record and kept live. Every fact carries where it came from, so nothing is asserted without a source. It gives away the public picture and charges for the intelligence and the private book, rather than reselling public data behind a paywall. And it does something no existing tool does: it puts the surface and the subsurface, real estate and minerals, ownership and production, on a single map.

## Where it starts

Reeves County first, proving the whole model end to end, then expanding across the Railroad Commission districts and into southeast New Mexico to cover the Permian, and outward from there. The goal is simple: the complete, trustworthy, living picture of the oil and gas record that the independent side of this business has never had, and the tools to act on it.
