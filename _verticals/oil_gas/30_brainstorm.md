# Oil and gas brainstorm, open space

> Open working space for the live brainstorm of what our tech can do for oil and gas, beyond the direct surface-to-backend mapping in `20_tech_to_og_map.md`. Seeded with prompts; meant to be filled in conversation. Nothing here is committed.

## The frame

We have the best backend for a land and asset product. The question this space explores: given the full stack (reasoning engine, jurisdictional spine, data rooms, payment rail, MCP surface), what is the complete set of things we could do for the oil and gas industry, including things neither prior vision imagined.

## Seed prompts (to work through live)

1. Beyond title and obligations, what oil and gas decisions are reasoning-over-jurisdictional-data problems that our engine is already shaped for? (Permitting timelines, surface-use conflict resolution, regulatory exposure scoring, lease-expiry risk.)
2. Midstream specifically (the WES shape): pipeline right-of-way and easement intelligence, produced-water disposal permitting, gathering-system regulatory compliance. What is the data spine for a midstream operator versus an upstream one?
3. The data room as a standalone product: a provenance-backed, revocable, role-scoped diligence room is valuable to anyone running an oil and gas transaction, independent of a marketplace. Is the data room the wedge rather than the whole platform?
4. Settlement as a service: multi-party payout on verified facts. Where else in oil and gas does money move on a fact that needs verification (joint-interest billing, revenue distribution, division-order payments)?
5. Agent-first delivery: what does an oil and gas MCP tool surface look like for an operator's own agents? (Query a lease, check an obligation, pull a run sheet, verify a title chain.) This is commitment 4 (MCP-first) applied to the vertical.
6. The calibration moat: oil and gas decisions have hard outcomes (a lease held or lost, a title cleared or disputed). That is exactly the outcome signal arrow-two needs. Does oil and gas calibrate the spine faster than real estate does?

## Constraints to keep honest

- Spine rule: anything we pursue has to feed or express Hauska, not become a separate oil and gas company.
- Cost-per-jurisdiction rule: oil and gas county and state onboarding has to hold the under-200-dollar-plus-one-hour target like every other jurisdiction.
- Sell reasoning, not data: the moat is calibrated reasoning over the data, never the raw third-party data we pass through at floor.
- Tenant sovereignty: a seller's or operator's private deal data never pools into a shared asset.
