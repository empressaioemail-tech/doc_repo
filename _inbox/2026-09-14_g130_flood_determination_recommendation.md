## The disagreement rate, with its denominator

The two stores the dispatch named — `atoms:flood-hazard-fact` (store A, per-parcel centroid
point-in-polygon) and `pls:node-facets:tier2` (store B, one live FEMA query per 0.005°-tile centre,
reused for every parcel in the tile) — were already swept for two counties on 2026-08-19
(`hauska-engine` `artifacts/ss-w11/flood-48021.json`, `flood-48453.json`, generated by SS-W11/P-45,
resolver `ss-w11/1.0.0`, source data `NFHL_48_20260101`):

- **Bastrop (48021):** roster 62,257. Both stores name a value for 62,249. **5,424 disagree — 8.71%
  of the comparable population.** (Over the full roster instead: 8.71% — the denominators happen
  to converge here because tier2 nearly fully covers Bastrop, one of its 10 baked counties.)
- **Travis (48453):** roster 380,918. Tier2 only covers 27,584 of them (7.2% — Travis is outside
  tier2's 10-county bake list for most of its area); only 4,145 parcels have an answer from *both*
  stores. Of those, **332 disagree — 8.01% of the comparable population**, but only **0.09% of the
  full roster**, because store B simply has no opinion on 92.8% of Travis.

**Excluded from both rates:** parcels either store lacks a row for at all (`one-sided-a`/
`one-sided-b`, the dominant class in Travis), and `absent-both`. The counting rule, stated in the
artifact itself: only entities where *both* stores name a value can arithmetically disagree: this
is the right population to rate against; a rate quoted over the full roster silently rewards the
sparser store for its own gaps.

**This ~8% figure is not the live headline any more** — see "What actually reaches the wire today"
below. It measures a disagreement between two stores, only one of which (A) still has any path to
a served surface, and even that only as a fallback for counties outside a program that, as of
today, covers all six counties this data was sampled from.

## Recommendation, in one sentence

**Ratify the parcel-record `flood` rail — a third, already-built, already-live derivation neither
store the dispatch named — as authoritative, and treat `atoms:flood-hazard-fact` as its sanctioned
legacy fallback for any county outside the parcel-record program; `pls:node-facets:tier2` should be
retired, since two independent ground-truth adjudications agree it loses essentially every real
disagreement, it structurally cannot carry a source vintage, and it already, deliberately, does not
reach any surface this investigation found.**

## The case against that recommendation

Parcel-record's flood rail is a *third* independent implementation of "which NFHL polygon contains
this parcel" — hauska-factory's `flood-ingest.mjs`, landed 2026-09-02, two weeks after the atom
writer it does not reuse and does not defer to. That is a third codebase computing nominally the
same fact, which is itself the failure shape this program has hit before (two independent,
non-syncing zoning-district pipelines, same repo pair, found 2026-09-09). Unlike the atom-vs-tier2
question, I did **not** independently re-adjudicate parcel-record's own served answers against a
fresh live NFHL query the way the existing C10 finding did for the other two stores (below) — my
confidence in it rests on reading its SQL and its writer's own stated defect-avoidance reasoning,
not on a fresh ground-truth sample of its output. That is a real, named gap in this recommendation's
evidence, not a hedge.

## What is actually right, and how that was established (not just measured — adjudicated)

Two independent, already-existing investigations both re-derived ground truth by re-querying live
FEMA NFHL (the actual agency ArcGIS service, not either store) and the bulk `tx_fema_nfhl_flood_zone`
table at the parcel's own point, and both concluded the same thing without citing each other's
numbers as evidence:

1. **`_inbox/2026-08-20_c10_flood_store_adjudication.md`** (worker C10, read-only, n=9 hand-adjudicated
   Bastrop parcels spanning all four disagreement classes). Verdict: *"The atom store is right about
   the parcel. The bake store is right about a different point (the tile centre), and wrong about the
   parcel."* Rejected, with evidence, three plausible alternative explanations before accepting this
   one: W-5 (atom's SFHA-flag defect) firing, W-3/W-4 (atom's centroid defects) firing, and FEMA
   edition drift. All three were checked against live+bulk NFHL at the parcel point and ruled out for
   this sample — the tile-centre mechanism alone explains every case, including the disagreement's
   own inverse (bake=X, atom=AE) and the older "AO/AE" framing, which C10 shows is the same tile
   mechanism, not a distinct subtype-merge problem.
2. **`ss-w11/flood-48021.json`'s own `adjudication` block** (2026-08-19, independently built, states
   explicitly it does not use C10's numbers or vice versa): re-queried ground truth at store A's own
   sample point for all 5,424 raw disagreements. Store A matched ground truth in **5,392 of 5,424
   (99.4%)**; store B matched in **30 of 5,424 (0.55%)**. Of the 5,424, 5,371 are fully
   `explained-by-sampling-point` (store A right, store B's tile centre up to 366 m away is simply a
   different, wrong, location) — median displacement 227 m, max 366 m. A residual 42
   are `split-subject` (parcel geometry genuinely straddles two NFHL polygons — the one class where
   B wins more often than A, 30-to-10, because a tile-centre sample can land in the *other* true zone
   of a boundary parcel while a centroid can miss the ring the same way); 11 stay
   `vintage-undecidable`.

**Second mechanism considered and rejected for the recommendation itself:** that store A's own known
defects — W-3 (vertex-mean centroid, not area centroid), W-4 (first-polygon-only on MultiPolygon),
W-5 (an SFHA string-flag match that treats anything other than literal `T`/`t`/`true` as non-SFHA,
including FEMA's own `"TRUE"`) — are why it wins these adjudications. Rejected for the *measured*
sample: C10 checked this directly (all nine sampled parcels are single-part, `ST_PointOnSurface` and
`ST_Centroid` agree in every case) and found none of the three fired. They remain **real, unmeasured
risk** for concave or multi-part parcels elsewhere in the roster — which is exactly why parcel-record's
independent writer (below) was built to route around all three rather than reuse the atom writer.

## Is the tier2 drop deliberate or incidental?

**Deliberate**, and it predates this dispatch. The exclusion is the same "anti-zombie" rule that
already blocks cortex-derived zoning and envelope from overwriting the atom chain's own product
truth (Master WDLL 3.7) — `tier2.flood` was folded into that same doctrine, not dropped by omission.
Nothing currently depends on tier2 being absent in the sense of a downstream consumer relying on its
absence as a signal; rather, nothing downstream calls it at all any more on the traced paths. Whether
`nodeFacetBakeTier2Cli.ts` (the writer) is still triggered by anything outside these six repos is
**unmeasured** — I found no Cloud Build/scheduler YAML in `legacy-design-tools` invoking it (an
`npm run node-facet-bake-tier2` script exists but nothing in-repo calls it), which is evidence of
absence, not proof; an external trigger this search cannot see would still be pure write-side cost
with no read-side effect on any surface traced above.

## What it would take to give the surviving source a vintage

Store A already has one: `editionsA` shows `NFHL_48_20260101` on 62,256 of 62,257 Bastrop rows and
380,917 of 380,918 Travis rows (99.998%+) — a real source edition, not a bake clock. Parcel-record's
flood cell also carries a real one (`source_vintage` from the NFHL table itself, plus `method:
"point-on-surface"`, both written per-cell by `flood-ingest.mjs` and passed through unchanged by
`floodHazardFactFromParcelRecord`'s `value` branch). Store B (tier2) cannot: both counties' own
`tier2VintageFieldAudit` show 100% of its `provenance.vintage` values are ISO instants (bake-run
timestamps), explicitly verdicted in the artifact itself as *"a BAKE CLOCK, not a source edition."*
There is no field on that store's write path this investigation found that could be repurposed into
a real edition without querying FEMA's panel-effective-date separately — which the new parcel-record
writer already flags as a known, honest gap (`panelEffectiveDateNote` in `flood-ingest.mjs`).
