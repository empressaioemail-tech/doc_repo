## The counting rule, at the point of use

The denominator for every percentage below is **the number of tier-1 bake rows
for that county**: one row per `place_key` matching `node:<fips>:%` under
`adapter_key = 'node-facets:tier1'`. That is the set a projection over the bake
could cover, and it is not the CAD row count, not the TxGIO parcel count, and
not the landing-table row count. Those three differ from it and from each other,
which is why the denominator is named here rather than assumed.

A rail counts as **present** only when a determination exists. Sentinels count
as UNMEASURED, never as present. Predicates are transcribed from the serve
path's own code at LDT `91e9086d`, read 2026-09-02:
`zoningDisposition` and `landUseDisposition` from
`artifacts/api-server/src/lib/r1BriefCompose.ts`, the punctuation-only situs
class from `artifacts/api-server/src/lib/situsCompose.ts`, and the flood and
special-district interpretations from `floodHazardFactRead.ts` and
`specialDistrictFactRead.ts`.

## Zoning is the rail whose honest coverage is not its present count

The bake carries a district on 12.3% of Bastrop. That number badly understates
what is answerable, because unincorporated Texas land carries no municipal
zoning, and that is a POSITIVE determination rather than a gap, but it takes
two independently derived inputs to earn it: the bake payload saying there is no
district, and `landing_parcel_jurisdiction` saying the parcel is outside every
city. Either input alone is not enough. An `in-city` parcel with no district is
a stamp gap, which is `unknown`.

| Bastrop | 9,556 | 50,157 | 18,086 | **76.8%** |
| Caldwell | 6,491 | 14,326 | 27,832 | **42.8%** |

| Travis | 233,236 | 89,987 | 177,084 | **64.6%** |
| Williamson | 228,639 | 105,043 | 268,368 | **55.4%** |

Zoning's evaluable share is between 42.8% and 76.8%, against a present count of
12.3% to 46.6%. The absent-verified half is between two and five times the size
of the present half, and it is the half that answers "unzoned land", which is
the land buyer's question far more often than "zoned SF-1" is.

**This is also the one place the projection and `get_smart_site` disagree, and
the disagreement is large.** `composeSmartSiteStub` derives zoning from ONE
input and `railStateFromSectionDisposition` maps `absent` to `unknown`, because
at section level an absence carries no positive typed result (the WDLL item 5
ruling). So for every parcel in the absent-verified column above,
`get_smart_site` reports `zoning: unknown` today while the projection would
report `absent-verified`. That is **50,157 parcels in Bastrop and 346,165 across
the six counties**: not a rounding difference, and item 2's check ("a parcel's
projected value matches `get_smart_site`") fails on exactly this class. It is
routed to the operator in the P-106 close rather than resolved by the lane, and
pinned by a named test so it cannot drift silently.

## Where the measurement contradicts the card

**1. The four dollar-and-age rails are at ZERO, in all six counties.**
The card lists market, land and improvement value and year built as in scope for
v1 and "cheap to project". They are not low; they are zero. `cad_property`
carries the values at source (the 2026-09-01 gap ledger measured them there),
and the tier-1 bake's `cadRoll` block does not: the `node-facet-patch-cad-roll`
CLI has not run for these counties. The card's own rule for item 2 is "built
from the baked facets, not re-derived from source", so reading `cad_property`
directly inside this card would be re-deriving. **These four rails are out of
the searchable set until the cadRoll patch runs.** The columns still exist in the
projection, unmeasured on every row, and every filter over them is refused,
which is what item 5's gate is for.

**2. ETJ has no source at all.** The deployment store carries
`tx_city_boundary` (1,222), `tx_county_boundary` (254), `tx_special_district`
(2,701) and `landing_parcel_jurisdiction` (981,405). Nothing carries an
extraterritorial-jurisdiction ring, and `landing_parcel_jurisdiction.disposition`
is CHECK-constrained to exactly `in-city | unincorporated | unresolved`. ETJ is
carried as a declared-ahead column per
`_decisions/2026-09-01_parcel_record_rails_v2_template.md`, state `unread` on
every row, filter always refused.

**3. landUse is zero in three of six counties** (Caldwell, Travis, and McLennan
at 34 parcels) and 84.9% in Williamson. A rail is not searchable per program; it
is searchable per county. This is exactly why item 5's refusal is keyed on
(rail, county) rather than on a global rail list.

**4. The card was RIGHT about the situs sentinel, to within 0.3 points.** It
predicted roughly 21 points of overstatement in Bastrop; measured 20.7. And the
measurement adds something the card did not say: the sentinel problem is
Bastrop-specific. Travis and Williamson are at 0.0 points, so a fleet-wide rule
derived from Bastrop would be over-fitted, while a per-county one is not.

## Two numbers that agree and should be reconciled rather than rounded off

In four of six counties, `acreage present`, `cityLimits present`,
`flood evaluable` and `specialDistrict evaluable` land on top of each other:
Bastrop 62,257 / 62,256 / 62,256 / 62,256 (spread 1); Caldwell 24,988 four times
(spread 0); Travis 380,918 / 380,917 / 380,917 / 380,917 (spread 1); McLennan
114,253 / 114,254 / 114,265 / 114,265 (spread 12). These are not four
independent measurements of four rails. They are one population, the parcels
the CAD join reached, measured four times.

The residuals are worth naming rather than rounding off. The one-parcel gaps are
the degenerate `prop_id = '0'` row, which `landing_parcel_jurisdiction` refuses
by CHECK constraint and the bake carries. McLennan's twelve is NOT explained by
that: it has ten more flood atoms and ten more special-district parcels than the
bake has rows, so atoms exist there for parcels the tier-1 bake does not carry.
That is a real, small, unexplained residual, and it is recorded here as
unexplained rather than absorbed into a percentage.

The consequence for the card: **filtering on acreage AND flood together does not
compound the coverage loss the way independent rails would.** In Bastrop the two
rails are evaluable on the same 62,256 parcels, so a two-rail filter is not
80% x 80%; it is 80%. That is good news, and it is only visible because the
numbers were reconciled instead of reported side by side. Hays (131,431 acreage
vs 116,420 city-limits) and Williamson (511,029 vs 282,570) do NOT follow the
pattern, so the rule is per county and not a program fact.

## The queries

Every predicate and every query text is in
`_inbox/2026-09-02_p106_rail_census.mjs`, which is the instrument that produced
this table. The two shapes that matter:

Bake rails, per county, one index range scan on
`place_layer_snapshots_adapter_place_uidx` (the database collation is `C.UTF-8`,
so a prefix `LIKE` is a range scan and not a sequential scan of 6.9M rows):

    with s as (
      select payload_json p from place_layer_snapshots
       where adapter_key = 'node-facets:tier1' and place_key like 'node:<fips>:%'
    )
    select count(*) as parcels,
           count(*) filter (where <rail predicate>) as "<rail>"
      from s

Atom rails, per county, one index-only range scan on
`atoms_entity_composite_unique`:

    select count(*) as rows,
           count(distinct regexp_replace(entity_id, '\.00000000$', '')) as distinct_parcels,
           count(*) filter (where body ? 'absence' or body->>'sourceTier' = 'absent'
                               or body ? 'verifiedAbsence') as typed_absence,
           ...
      from atoms
     where entity_type = 'flood-hazard-fact' and entity_id like '<fips>:%'

## What this table says the searchable set is

Searchable in v1, on the evidence above: **acreage**, **flood** (with the SFHA
flag), **cityLimits**, **specialDistrict**, and **zoningDistrict**, the last
one only once its absent-verified half is counted, which needs the two-input
derivation.

Per-county, and only per-county: **landUse**, in Hays and Williamson.

Out of v1: **marketValue**, **landValue**, **improvementValue**, **yearBuilt**
(zero everywhere) and **etj** (no source). They keep their columns and their
refusals.
