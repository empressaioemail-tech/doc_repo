## 1. The four steps per (city, district) unit — from scope 4.1

1. **Locate the governing instrument and its effective date at source.** Most-current source wins
   (`_decisions/2026-09-11_setback_source_most_current_wins.md`). The date is READ at source: an
   ordinance adoption or effective date, or an ArcGIS `editingInfo.lastEditDate`. It is never assumed
   from the source kind. **An unreadable date is a conflict row carrying both values, never a pick.**
2. **Determine what the official map is** (a staged GIS layer, a PDF, or a GIS-kept map published as
   a PDF).
3. **Find the endpoint, or fail and record the sources tried.**
4. **Extract the dimensional table verbatim with its citation and date**: front, side, rear, corner,
   height, coverage, footprint.

## 2. Classify each city (scope 2b)

`(1)` zoned with a staged layer; `(2a)` zoned, with an undocumented queryable endpoint to find;
`(2b)` zoned, official map published only as a PDF; `(3)` unzoned. A category-3 city is a
**declaration** for P-156, not a table.

## 3. The rules, and they are not advisory

- **NEVER FABRICATE.** A code that is not in the ordinance is a note, not a row. A value you did not
  read verbatim from a source you actually fetched does not get written. If the ordinance is silent,
  write the `not_specified` sentinel with a citation to the section you searched and found silent —
  an honest gap, never an interpolation.
- **"Not found" lists every source tried**: Municode, eCode360, American Legal, the city's own
  document centre, and an ArcGIS REST search. A negative carries its basis; an empty search result is
  not an absence.
- **PUD / PDD / PD / PC codes get no Euclidean row.** They are A-164's PUD message (P-256/P-257) and
  your worklist marks those units out of scope already. If you find one you believe is a real
  Euclidean district, say so in your fragment with evidence rather than writing a row.
- **No store writes. No merges. No deploys.** You push a branch and open a PR; you do not merge it.
- **Do not change a zoning adapter.** Austin's source layer and base-code parser are P-259's.
- **Two-letter codes are real districts somewhere**: Austin's `RR` is Rural Residence.
- State a ratio with its counting rule, next to the number. Report what IS.

## 5. Priority and budget

Work **largest parcel count first** within your assignment (the worklist carries the counts). Do not
spend your whole budget on one city or one code: a lane that returns 20 verified rows and 30 honest
`blocked` entries beats a lane that returns 3 rows and silence. If a source turns out to be
unreachable, record the attempt and move on; four failed attempts on one target means stop and
report, not retry.

Watch for these two failure modes, both measured on this program:
- **A plausible value nobody checked.** The belton note records genuine ordinance typos preserved
  verbatim rather than silently corrected, and one internally ambiguous clause coded at reduced
  confidence with the ambiguity called out. Do that, rather than picking the reading that looks
  tidier.
- **A silent fallback.** An empty result is not an absence. If you did not find the city's ordinance,
  the state is `blocked` with the sources tried, not an empty table that serves as if the city had no
  rules.
