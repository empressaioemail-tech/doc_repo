# Positioning and brand: the live layer inside Smart Site

Governing ruling (2026-08-06, `convergence_smart_site_live_layer.md`): this is not a product with its own brand. It is what a claimed Smart Site does for its owner. Every piece of language below serves that single position. When a draft of copy starts treating "the watch app" or "the sensor platform" as a named thing a customer buys, the draft has drifted; pull it back to the site.

## The position

For someone who owns or operates real property (a home, a portfolio of buildings, a city's infrastructure), Smart Site already holds the record of their place: assembled, verified, cited. The live layer is the upgrade that makes that record aware of the present: the site knows its own current state, watches for what threatens it, and tells the right person while there is still time to act. The customer is not buying sensors, dashboards, or an app. They are giving their site senses, and putting watches on what matters.

The differentiated claims, each earned by architecture we actually have: every alert says why, with source, confidence, and time, not just that something happened. Everything the site learns accumulates on a durable record the owner keeps (the event ledger, the maintenance history, the equipment inventory), instead of evaporating in a vendor's notification feed. And the owner's private data stays theirs: it is never pooled, never sold, never visible to anyone they did not authorize, enforced at the data layer.

## One journey, one grammar

EXPLORE (anyone can see any site's public record) → CLAIM (take ownership of your site's twin) → TWIN IT (capture what you have, connect what already reports, add senses where it is blind) → WATCH IT (freeze watch, water watch, energy watch, maintenance watch).

This grammar is the pitch. The pain opens (every winter, pipes burst in buildings that had no idea they were cold); the twin explains (your building, with a record of itself, awake); the watch closes (a freeze watch on your building, watching every riser, telling your staff before the pipe goes). Concrete pains, named watches, the customer's own building in the close.

## Vocabulary rules

The vocabulary is the brand. Use it exactly; it does the customer education by itself.

- A SMART SITE is the place. A TWIN is the site's record of itself. TWIN is also the verb: you twin a building.
- SENSES, not "IoT devices," "hardware," or "sensor network" in customer copy. Sensors are the twin's senses; the word "sensor" is fine when speaking about a physical device, but the system is never "a sensor platform."
- A WATCH is a named, plain-English unit: freeze watch, water watch, energy watch, maintenance watch, bearing watch. Lowercase, descriptive, composable. Never "monitoring solution," "alerting module," or an acronym.
- LIVE is the state a twinned site reaches. Cold/warm/live is internal vocabulary; externally say the site is live, or has senses, and let the ladder stay backstage.
- The FIELD COMPANION is the staff-facing surface for operators; homeowners just have their site.
- Radio protocols, gateway architecture, and network servers never appear in customer-facing copy. They are install-spec detail.

Consumer articulation: "Twin your home" is the interim line and is expected to be renamed (candidate: "property watch"). Until renamed, use it only as a call to action, never as a product name.

## Claims discipline

Sayable, each traceable to record: property owners and cities are asking for this (two customer-originated asks on file, 2026-08-02 and 2026-08-06). Delivered today as a scoped pilot engagement. Every alert carries its source, confidence, and timestamp. Your data is yours: private telemetry is never pooled into anyone else's product, enforced at the data layer, and a city's view is built from the city's own infrastructure, never from private buildings.

Not sayable: a shipped, buy-it-today product tier (doc 41 honesty rule; it is an engagement until it is not); any delivery date; "prevents" as an absolute (we give early warning and a durable record; the pipe is saved by the person we alerted); dollar-savings or cycle-time figures (consistent with the SmartCity approved-claims register); anything that reads as a security or protection product (the SCADA record's rule); anything adjacent to care monitoring, fall detection, or resident surveillance. "We watch the building, not the people" remains an engineering scope rule, not a tagline, per the 2026-08-06 ruling; in an assisted-living room, state scope honestly when asked rather than marketing the boundary.

The insurance and documentation angle (event ledger as claims-grade record) may be described as what the record is good for; never promise premium outcomes.

## Brand mechanics

Entity and family: this is Empressa product surface, inside the Smart Site line. The substrate is never named in customer copy. The smart-* family convention holds (smart-* names twinned places and things an owner holds); watches are capabilities of a smart site, so they do not take the smart- prefix. No new logos, no sub-brand marks.

Visual: inherit the Smart Site application's visual system (the current PE rebrand tokens) for anything product-shaped. Field materials (install guides, site notes sheets, printed QR tags) may use the utility field style established in the pilot install guide (pipe-marker labeling, station codes, high-contrast instructional diagrams); that style is for the trade-facing field context only and does not migrate into marketing surfaces.

Voice: plain words, short sentences, the building in every example. The reader should never need the word "platform" to understand what they get. If a sentence works with "system" deleted, delete it.

## Offer architecture (ratified 2026-08-06)

The free hook maps to Layer 1 of the standing tier model: forecast-only freeze watch is free for any claimed site (public data only: NWS joined to the public record twin). The paid line is crossed exactly where the tier model already draws it: private data. Senses, measured watches, the inventory, the ledger are Layer 2.

The unit of purchase is the WATCH, per site, never per sensor. Per-sensor pricing punishes coverage, an anti-proactive incentive that would push customers to skip exactly the systemic senses that make the product work. Package shape: TWIN IT is a one-time capture engagement (site records, inventory, shutoff map, senses installed; priced as real work). WATCH IT is the per-site recurring relationship (freeze, water, energy sold by name; bundle is "the building watch"; maintenance watches included with the inventory as stickiness, not revenue). HARDWARE is pass-through at transparent cost and owned by the customer: hardware-is-yours is the commercial twin of data-is-yours, and it means kit margin is never the business, keeping us indifferent to kit size and honest about right-sizing.

Segments: operator (pilot-first engagement converting to per-building subscription; portfolio view and field companion included), assisted living (identical package, ledger-as-documentation emphasis), homeowner (self-serve, deliberately later), city (never off a price list; custom engagement per the SCADA ruling).

Sales conversation within claims discipline: we print no savings figures; the buyer supplies the number ("what did last winter cost you?") and the close is their own arithmetic next to a per-building subscription. The ledger closes second (a record the insurer, board, and refinance all wish existed). Two channels: the architect channel (the descriptor-shaped catalog lands sensors as a schedule on a drawing sheet, so architects can spec the live layer into new construction; buildings born twinned) and the install guide itself as a sales asset (your own staff installs it in an hour, killing the integration-project objection).

Dollar figures, pilot terms, and tier fine print are bizops and stay out of this doc.
