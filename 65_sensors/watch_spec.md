# Watch spec

A watch is the unit of sensor intelligence: a standing rule on a live twin. This doc is the contract every vertical shares. The multifamily watches (freeze, water, energy), the municipal bearing watch on a pump station, and any future oil and gas watch are all instances of this one shape. The contract is specified here first, exposed as API and MCP tools second, and consumed by the human surface (`watch_app_spec.md`) third. That ordering is how a UI-heavy product line honors the MCP-first commitment: the agent-consumable contract is the product; the app is a client of it.

## Anatomy of a watch

A watch binds five things:

TARGET. The node or node-set on the twin the watch reads: a riser, a mechanical room, a meter, the whole building filtered by occupancy state. Targets are addressed by node id, never by sensor id; sensors come and go, nodes persist.

BASELINE. What normal looks like for the target. A baseline may be asserted at setup (pipe temp should sit above 45 degrees) or learned from accumulated history (this building's weather-normalized energy curve). Every baseline carries provenance saying which it is, consistent with the confidence-is-earned commitment: asserted with provenance at first, earned through accumulation, never a bare number presented as earned.

CONDITION. The deviation that fires: a threshold crossing, a trajectory (falling through 38 degrees), a pattern (continuous flow during a should-be-quiet window), an external signal joined to the record (forecast low under threshold for a building with exposed runs), or elapsed time or runtime (a maintenance watch: filter change due at 90 days, service due at rated runtime hours). Maintenance watches make the equipment inventory actionable: every schedule line generated by the app's label-capture flow is a watch with a time condition, its completions logged as events, so a building's maintenance history accumulates on the twin with the same acknowledgment discipline as everything else.

SEVERITY TIER. Two tiers in v1. INFORMATIONAL: worth knowing, batched, no escalation. ACT-NOW: someone goes to the building. Tier assignment is part of the watch config, and one watch may emit at both tiers (a freeze watch pre-alerts informationally on forecast and escalates to act-now on measured pipe temp).

ROSTER. Who gets told, in what order, through what channels, and what happens on no acknowledgment (escalate to the next name after a configured interval). The roster is a record on the twin, not app configuration, so it survives any particular surface.

## Events and the ledger

When a condition fires, the watch emits an EVENT onto the twin. An event is durable and atomized: it carries the watch id, the target node, the condition that fired, the evidence (readings, forecast values, baseline at fire time), source, timestamp, confidence, and access policy. Notifications reference the event; acknowledgments and resolutions append to it.

The event lifecycle: OPEN (fired, roster notified), ACKNOWLEDGED (a named person accepted it, stamp on the ledger), RESOLVED (a named person closed it with an outcome), EXPIRED (condition cleared without human action, recorded honestly as such). The acknowledgment trail is a first-class product output: for an assisted living operator, "alert 18:04, acked by J. 18:11, walkthrough complete 19:30" is close to compliance-grade documentation of response, and it lives on the building's record permanently.

OUTCOME is the calibration payload. A resolved event records what was actually found (pipe froze or held, leak confirmed or false alarm, equipment fault confirmed or dismissed). Predicted against measured, per event, per building, is arrow two operating on physical infrastructure. Watch quality is measurable from the ledger: catch rate, false-alarm rate, lead time. Those numbers are the earned confidence a mature watch carries.

## Notification plane

Channels in v1: push (via the app), SMS, email. Two rules are load-bearing. First, act-now never depends on a single channel; the act-now tier always sends push plus SMS. Second, notification delivery and acknowledgment are themselves recorded on the event, so "staff was told" is a fact with a timestamp, not an assumption.

Quiet hours, batching windows for the informational tier, and per-person channel preferences are roster-level configuration.

## External signals

Watches may join external feeds to the record. v1 signals: NWS hourly forecast (freeze pre-alert, water pre-alert, degree-day normalization for energy baselines) and the FEMA flood layer already served in the record plane. External signals are sources like any other: their readings carry source and timestamp, and a condition built on a forecast says so in its evidence.

## API and MCP surface

The watch plane exposes, at minimum: query twin state (nodes with current derived state), list and read events, acknowledge and resolve events, list watches and their configs, and the commissioning operations the app's installer flow consumes (bind sensor to node, confirm first reading). The same contract is exposed as MCP tools so an agent operator can read a building's state and event history the same way a human reads the app. Tool naming, gate placement, and auth posture follow the existing MCP server conventions and are settled at implementation time, not here.
