# Stub thesis: the verified franchise economy

**Framing additions (Nick, 2026-08-13, same session):** this is a **digital economy model, blending humans and tech** — the human twins are constitutive, not a feature: every member account is a person's living record (fan twin), every verified athlete is a human twin earning from their own record, and the economy is people, places, and value twinned, verified, and transacting on one platform. Tagline coined for Empressa: **"we build digital economies."** Candidate positioning line for the brand; decision-log at close if ratified.

## The move

The crypto-native league model does not belong to any league. It is our system. AF1 is offered the first design-partner seat, on the same pattern the portfolio has already run twice: Bastrop as the design-partner city for SmartCity (pioneering city in a network, never a data source), and the ATX Bulls as the design-partner team for the fan platform. We own the model, the template, and every refinement; partners get the flagship position, input into the design, and partner economics. The model then serves other sports, other leagues, and other economies of the same shape.

## The abstraction (what the system actually is)

Strip sports away and the system is a machine for running a **verified franchise economy**: any network with a center, nodes, members, and anchorable assets.

| Role | Sports instance | The general role |
|---|---|---|
| Center | the league (AF1) | issuer and governor: owns the network brand, grants territories, sets conduct rules, issues the capital layer |
| Nodes | teams | operators: run the local business on the shared template |
| Members | fans | the demand engine: accounts with status, history, and property; consumptive instruments only |
| Anchored assets | athletes (verified twins), venues (born-twin, league-owned) | the real-world referents that give the capital layer something true to point at |

Three layers, per the corrected 2026-08-13 architecture (recorded in `_prospects/atx_bulls/13_af1_league_pitch.md`):

1. **Member layer** (per node, non-tradable, consumptive): accounts, founding passes, memberships, drops, twin content. The business the token points at.
2. **Capital layer** (center-issued, tradable): master token with per-node sub-tokens, linked to assets the center owns (brand, territory), structured deliberately on the securities side by a tokenomics team plus counsel. Candidate shapes: reserve/settlement master (Chiliz pattern), parent-with-series, or one-contract-many-IDs.
3. **RWA enhancer**: capital-layer liquidity funds center-owned real assets (stadiums in the sports instance), nodes assemble the projects, each asset links to its node's sub-token with the twin as continuous verified disclosure. The center becomes asset-owning.

The load-bearing joint across all three is verification: sub-token linkage claims (member-graph size, venue state, verified performance assets) must be continuously attestable, and the attestation feed is the spine doing what it already does. Tokens need twins; here that is the capital structure, not a slogan.

## Built off what exists today

Every layer maps to running machinery: member accounts are the fan platform built for the Bulls (and the human-twin pattern); verified subjects are the A1 athlete program and the venue twins (originate-mode per doc 42); disclosure rooms are generalized Smart Files; payments, splits, and metering are the rails; attestations are atoms with provenance. The tokenomics team (forming, 2026-08-13) designs the capital instrument; we operate the truth it points at and the businesses that give it cash flow. Clean seam, both sides needed, ours is the one that compounds.

This expresses the Hauska spine rule rather than escaping it: the system is the referent layer productized for network economies, and every partner economy deposits verification demand back into the substrate.

## Who else has this shape

Beyond sports leagues: franchise business systems (franchisor, franchisees, customers, locations), venue and hospitality groups, music labels and artist rosters, university and church networks, city networks (SmartCity itself is the center-and-nodes shape with citizens as members). The sports instance is first because the Bulls and AF1 are live, arena economics are small enough to move fast, and the fan-passion layer makes the member economy vivid. The generalization is the reason the model must stay in-house.

## The design-partner offer (AF1 shape)

What AF1 gets: the flagship league position and its press; input into the template while it is still forming; partner economics on the platform; the Bulls already running the node template inside their league. What AF1 gives: the proving ground, the reference, conduct-rule adoption, and the case study. What AF1 does not get: ownership of the model, the template, or refinements. If AF1 declines the seat, the model loses nothing but its first logo; the Bulls node keeps proving the member layer regardless, and the seat is offered to the next network.

## The refinement flywheel

The portfolio's two-spine method, generalized: every partner economy is an implementation of the model, and implementation breakthroughs feed the model, not the partner's fork. The template each new economy receives is the hardened residue of every prior deployment. This is the same reason the atom contract survived two spines; the franchise economy model is designed to survive N economies from birth.
