Section 0 · Positioning

The system of record, not the dashboard about it

SmartCity looks like the city’s book of record with an operator’s console around it: hairline rules instead of floating cards, identifiers set in mono, color spent only on what is unresolved, and nothing on screen a clerk could not defend in a public meeting.

Internal · do not publish outside the building

This page carries citations to a licensed corpus under a proof-of-concept licence that does not extend to customer-facing applications. Internal circulation only. If any mock leaves the building, strip the licensed corpus titles first.

Internal one-liner

Quiet surfaces, loud exceptions, honest absence.

Three things follow from that sentence and drive every decision below. First, the metaphor is the register, not the card deck. A city runs on rows that carry an identifier, a date, a status, and a person. That is what the eye should land on. Second, color is budgeted. The old app spent its color on decoration and had none left for meaning; here, a satisfied thing is gray and an unresolved thing is the loudest object on the screen. Third, absence is a designed state. A city that has not connected its ledger sees a sentence explaining why, not a $0.

Applies to four first-class products on one system: Dashboards, Smart Files, Plan Review, and Asset Management. One token set, one shell, four products. None of the four is a later mount or a phase two.

Section 1 · Operating laws

Five laws

These settle arguments. If a screen breaks one, the screen is wrong, not the law.

  • 1One second to orient. Every screen answers where am I, what needs me, and what is missing — in that order, before any scrolling. City identity and environment sit top-left, exceptions sit in the page header, absence is stated in place.
  • 2One of everything. One type ramp, one 4px spacing scale, one radius family, one shadow ladder, one accent. A second sans, a second accent, or a nested product header is a defect.
  • 3Quiet on satisfied, loud on unresolved. Pass is gray. Uncertain and Unchecked carry the strongest treatment on the page. This inverts the usual instinct and it is deliberate: a plans examiner is paid to find the unresolved rows, not to admire the passing ones.
  • 4Records, not feeds. A connected system becomes rows with provenance. Every value that came from somewhere carries source and last-read. Nothing renders as a vendor’s branded widget.
  • 5Absence is stated, never simulated. No $0 budget, no “Payment complete”, no placeholder count. Empty says what is missing and on what basis.

Color laws

  • ✓Status color lives only in status carriers: the pill, the 3px row rail, the matrix row, an icon. Never a card background, never a border on a whole panel, never text of a subject line.
  • ✓Two colored things per region, maximum. A tile carrying four accents is the single loudest symptom of the old app.
  • ✓Status is never color alone. Pill = glyph + word + color. Row rail always pairs with a pill in the row.
  • ×No gradients on surfaces, no glow, no backdrop-filter glass, no colored shadows. Elevation is not a mood.
  • ×The map is the one place saturation may concentrate. On a map screen, no other element gets a colored fill.

2.2 Typography

The old app ran IBM Plex Sans in the layout and named Inter in the components. The fix is not to pick a winner between two sans faces — it is to delete the second sans and give the survivor a real job. Two faces, two jobs:

  • AInter — --sc-font-ui. All interface text: nav, labels, buttons, prose, subject lines. Chosen for dense UI legibility at 13–14px and for its tabular figures, not for fashion.
  • BIBM Plex Mono — --sc-font-data. Identifiers only, and identifiers always. Case numbers, parcel IDs, code sections, timestamps, money, counts, section labels. This is the souvenir worth keeping: it is the typewriter of the record, and it makes an identifier scannable in a column without any color at all.

The rule that keeps it honest: if a person would read it aloud as a number or a code, it is mono; if they would read it aloud as a sentence, it is Inter. No third face. No display face. A municipal console does not need a personality face, and one would read as marketing.

The elevation rule

In light, elevation is a shadow. In dark, elevation is a surface step — a panel lifts by moving surface → surface-2 → surface-3, and the shadow only deepens the ground beneath a true overlay. A resting panel has no shadow in either theme; it is defined by its 1px border. This is what kills the floating-glass look at the root.

2.4 Motion

Three durations, one easing curve, and a short list of things allowed to move.

  • —--sc-dur-1: 100ms hover, press, chip toggle, focus ring.
  • —--sc-dur-2: 140ms popover, dropdown, tooltip, toast in.
  • —--sc-dur-3: 180ms drawer and dialog only. Nothing in the system is slower.
  • ×Data never animates. No counting-up numbers, no row-enter stagger, no chart draw-in, no LED pulse, no shimmer. A number that animates is a number a city manager cannot read at a glance.
  • ✓Loading is a static skeleton with real row geometry. Spinners are 16px and inline only. Anything over two seconds gets a determinate bar with what it is doing.
  • ✓prefers-reduced-motion zeroes every duration. Already in the stylesheet, not an afterthought.
  • !Named exception, Compass only. Present, dismiss and maximize are a shared-element transition on --sc-spring = stiffness 320, damping 32, mass 0.9, settling near 400ms, which reads in the 350 to 500ms band. Nothing else inherits it. Drawers, dialogs, row enters, popovers and toasts stay on --sc-dur-1 through --sc-dur-3, and data still never animates. Reduced motion gives an instant present with no spring and no scale. The exception exists because the sheet is not an element appearing on top of the page; it is the source control changing size, and 180ms is too short to read as continuous geometry.

2.5 Grid and density

Fixed chrome, fluid content. The shell is topbar 52 / nav 248 (rail 56 collapsed) / page header 68–96 / context rail 380. Content is a 12-column fluid grid with a 24px comfortable gutter and 16px compact. Work surfaces run to a 1600px cap; reading surfaces (a finding, a determination, a citizen page) cap at 720px.

Section 3 · App shell

Sidebar, and the reason is arithmetic

Recommendation: a persistent left sidebar for navigation, with a thin top bar for identity and search. The old app used a seven-item horizontal nav, and seven is already its ceiling. This product line has to hold a lens roster that grows by department (development services, finance, public works, police, fire, fleet), plus Plan Review as its own staff path, plus Asset Management and Smart Files mounts later. A top nav absorbs that by growing an overflow menu, which is where features go to die. A sidebar absorbs it by grouping, holds a count badge per item without crowding, collapses to a 56px icon rail when a map or a plan sheet needs the width, and gives every lens a stable vertical position a person learns in a week.

The top bar is not navigation. It carries four things and nothing else: which city, which environment, search, and who you are. That is the one-second orientation answer, and it is why it sits above everything.

3.1 The environment badge

Demo, live city, and next city must never read the same. Three identities that look identical is how a demo fixture gets quoted in a council meeting as a real number. The badge sits immediately after the city name, is dashed for anything not a live city, and is the only place amber appears in the chrome.

Section 6 · Prohibitions

Anti-patterns, named

Each of these is in the current Bastrop staff dashboard. Naming them makes them reviewable: a pull request that reintroduces one can be rejected by number.

A1 — Do not: hero PNG and greeting

A generated topographic image, “Good Morning, Nick”, and an All Systems Operational LED consume the entire first screen and answer no question a city manager has.

A1 — Instead

Page header: city, lens, and four clickable exception numbers. The first scroll-free thing below it is the list of what needs a person today.

A2 — Do not: LED pulse and glow

Pulsing dots, teal glow, and backdrop-filter glass cards. Motion that never stops trains staff to ignore motion, which is the one channel a real alert needs.

A2 — Instead

Static status pill with glyph and word. Panels are defined by a 1px border. Shadow only under a true overlay. Nothing on a resting screen moves.

A3 — Do not: nested product header

PermitFlow’s slate-900 and blue-400 chrome living inside the app, with its own nav. Two headers means the user has to work out which product they are in.

A3 — Instead

One shell. Plan Review is a nav item and a set of regions inside it, on these tokens. There is never a second header, a second nav, or a second accent.

A4 — Do not: iframe as a page

A foreign site framed as a workspace, carrying its own chrome, its own persona gate, and its own visual language into ours.

A4 — Instead

Native regions inside our page header. If a surface cannot render in our frame with our header, it is not ready to be part of the product.

A5 — Do not: 9px type

9px and 10px labels, unreadable at desk distance, used to fit more widgets into a screen that already had too many.

A5 — Instead

12px floor, enforced. If content will not fit at 12px, the screen is carrying too much — cut content, do not shrink type.

A6 — Do not: four accents on one tile

Teal, green, amber and purple on a single card, so none of them means anything and the eye has nowhere to land.

A6 — Instead

Two colored things per region, maximum. Color only in status carriers. If everything is flagged, nothing is.

A7 — Do not: vendor-feed wallpaper

A mosaic of vendor widgets and a hardcoded “7 integrations · Connected” badge that stays green whether or not anything was read.

A7 — Instead

Connected systems become rows with provenance and a real last-read time. The footer states 4 of 6 sources read and the stale one says so.

A8 — Do not: zero theater

A $0 budget, an empty chart, or a “Payment complete” screen behind a setTimeout. Each one is a false claim rendered in the city’s name.

A8 — Instead

Honest-empty with a basis block, and a real-world alternative where one exists. Absence is a designed state, not a rendering accident.

A9 — Do not: metrics above the work

Six hero metric cards pushing the queue below the fold, so the surface reads as a report about the work rather than the work.

A9 — Instead

Metric strip inside the page header: plain numbers, no cards, maximum four, each one filters the queue that starts immediately below.

A10 — Do not: two sans faces

IBM Plex Sans in the layout and Inter in the components, so weights and metrics drift screen to screen and nothing looks deliberate.

A10 — Instead

One sans for language, one mono for identifiers. Read it aloud as a number and it is mono; read it aloud as a sentence and it is Inter.
