# Where your intelligence runs

**Client-facing. Cloud by default, local when you want it, and what it costs as AI gets more expensive.**

## What the hardware does, and why it matters

The intelligence layer we build for you needs somewhere to think. Two things run on that hardware. The first is your spine: the structured, compounding record of everything your business produces, which you own. The second is the reasoning that runs against it: the models that draft your variance commentary, catch the water bill that drifted out of line, and answer the questions your team asks.

Where that hardware sits is your decision, and it is a real lever, not a technicality. It controls three things you care about: who can physically touch your data, how much your AI costs to run, and whether you are locked into anyone. The point of giving you the choice is the same point as the rest of what we build. You stay in control of the asset.

## The default is cloud

For most of what you do, the cloud is the right answer and it is where we start. You get the best available models on day one, nothing to buy, nothing to rack, and capacity that scales the moment you need more of it. Your data stays isolated to you, governed by role-based access enforced at the data layer. For the vast majority of multifamily operators, a well-run cloud deployment is where the intelligence layer should live, and you should be skeptical of anyone who tells you that you must buy hardware to get started. You do not.

## If you want it local, here is the way

Some operators want some or all of the intelligence running inside their own walls. The reasons are legitimate. A board that has made data sovereignty a priority. Regulated or sensitive data that should not leave the building. A preference to own the box outright rather than rent the compute forever. If that is you, the path is real and it is no longer expensive or exotic.

The entry point is a single desk-side appliance, about the size of a hardback book, that holds a large model entirely in its own memory and runs your day-to-day intelligence locally. As of 2026 a capable one is roughly the price of a high-end laptop, not a server room. You can put one in an office. You can put several in a small cluster. For heavier workloads there is a workstation tier, and for a large operation a full on-prem deployment. You choose how far down that path to go, and you can start in the cloud and move workloads local over time without redoing the work.

The honest division of labor is this. The local box is excellent for the high-volume, private, everyday work: the routine drafting, the flagging, the lookups your team runs constantly. The deepest reasoning, the hardest planning questions, still runs best on the strongest models, which live in the cloud and are called only when the question is worth it. A good design uses both. The private, repetitive work stays close to home and cheap, and the rare hard question reaches for the best model available. You are not choosing one or the other. You are putting each kind of work where it belongs.

What does not change in any of this is ownership. Local or cloud, the spine is yours, the data leaves with you if you ever leave, and the hardware decision never becomes a lock-in.

## What it costs in a world where every token gets more expensive

Here is the part worth thinking about now rather than later. The current price of cloud AI is not the natural price of it. A great deal of today's AI is sold below cost while the industry competes for position. As that settles, the per-use price of the strongest models is more likely to rise than to fall. If your entire operation is wired directly to a meter that someone else controls, you are exposed to that.

The intelligence layer reduces that exposure in two ways, and they compound.

The first is structural, and it is true even if you never buy a single piece of hardware. Most of the cost of an AI answer is the work of going out, finding the relevant information, and reading it from scratch every single time. We do that work once. When information enters your spine it is structured and pre-digested, so the next time a question touches it the model reads a clean, compact answer instead of re-deriving it. The same question costs a fraction of the compute it would otherwise. The more your spine compounds, the wider that gap gets. So as the price per unit of AI rises, the amount of AI you have to spend to run your business falls.

The second is the hardware choice. Running the everyday work on a box you own converts a variable bill you do not control into a fixed cost you do. You buy the appliance once. The routine intelligence it produces does not show up on a monthly meter that can be repriced against you. You keep the cloud for the hard questions, where paying for the best model is worth it, and you stop paying cloud rates for the routine work that does not need them.

Put together: you are not betting on cloud AI staying cheap. You are building so that it does not matter much if it does not. The structure makes each answer cost less, and owning the box for the routine work caps your exposure to whatever the meter does next. That is the hedge, and it gets stronger every month the spine compounds, not weaker.

## What you own at the end

The same thing that is true everywhere else in what we build is true here. You own the spine. You decide where it runs. You can move it. You are not locked in to us or to any cloud, and the hardware question is one more place where the control stays with you.
