1 of 12 rules was actually evaluated. 1 was escalated. 10 were not evaluated.
One evaluated rule is not a failure of honesty, it is the honest number: exactly one adjudicator is built. The ten are broken out below on two axes that must never be added together, and both appear in the applicant’s letter.
Our corpus could not supply the rule 9 · four kinds, from the service’s own absence taxonomy
absent-verified 2
Read the adopted edition and confirmed the section is not in it.The strong claim. There is no such requirement in this jurisdiction, and we looked. Safe to stop worrying about.
not-entitled 1
Behind a licence this product does not hold.The section is cited and its text is not reproduced. We will not paraphrase a code we are not licensed to quote.
source-unavailable 1
Ingested, but the body could not be retrieved on this run.A transient fault on our side, not a statement about the code. Re-running may resolve it.
unchecked 5
Never ingested into our corpus.A statement about what we hold, not about the code book. The requirement may well apply. A human must check it.
These four are the reason a reviewer can trust the one. `unchecked` is a claim about OUR CORPUS; `absent-verified` is a claim about the CODE. Collapsing them would let a gap in our ingest read as a clean bill of health.
A different axis 1 · nothing to do with the corpus
No adjudicator is built for this rule.
Permitted use is cited and readable. It is not a numeric comparison, so there is nothing to adjudicate a proposal against. The corpus is fine; the reasoning does not exist yet.
Counted apart from the nine above on purpose. A corpus gap and a reasoning gap are different problems with different owners.
And one the taxonomy cannot describe the whole chain, not a section
If the corpus itself is unreachable, that is one row, not twelve.
A whole-chain fetch failure must not be rendered as twelve per-section absences. Twelve identical failures read as twelve findings about the code; they are one finding about us.
