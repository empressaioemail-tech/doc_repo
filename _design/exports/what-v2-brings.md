# What the new SmartCity OS lets your staff do

A plain summary of what changes day to day, compared with the system you use now.

---

## The short version

Today your staff look at dashboards, then go somewhere else to do the work. In the new system
**the dashboard is the work**. What you see is what you click, and what you click is what you
act on.

---

## Start the day knowing what needs you

The first screen answers three questions: what needs a decision, what is overdue, and what is
coming this week.

Not a wall of charts. A short list of things waiting on a person, greatest pressure first —
plan reviews past their due date, permits about to expire, meetings in the next seven days. A
department head can open it, see the six things that need them, and start.

## Click a number and land on the work

Every figure on the page is a doorway. "41 work orders overdue" is not a statistic you note and
act on later. Click it and you are looking at those 41 work orders, already filtered, ready to
assign or close.

This is the single biggest change in how the system feels. There is no step between seeing a
problem and working it.

## Work a queue without leaving the page

Permits, inspections, work orders, code enforcement and licences all behave the same way: one
consistent table you can search, filter by status or type, sort, page through and export.

Learn it once on permits and you already know how inspections works.

You can also see **who is carrying what** — how many open inspections each inspector has, how
many cases each code officer is working — so assignment is a decision rather than a guess.

## See everything about an address together

The map sits beside your work rather than on a separate page. Open a property and you get the
parcel, its zoning, its permits, its inspections and its cases in one place. Widen the map when
you need it, push it aside when you do not, and never lose your place in the list.

## Answer the drainage question without commissioning a report

When it rains four inches, what happens to this property? Today that takes a phone call, a wait,
and often a paid engineering study. Now it is on screen in minutes, and you can compare one inch
against four side by side.

It does not replace your engineer. It tells you whether you need one — which is a budget decision
as much as a technical one.

And it screens every permit currently in review at once, ranked by which sit on ground that
ponds, so a reviewer sees the three worth a closer look before opening any of them.

## Review a plan and issue the letter from the same place

Open a submittal and you see the drawing, the findings, and the code behind each one. Every
finding names the **actual code section** it came from, and the applicant's correction notice
carries the same citation.

A review that takes two weeks today gets a first pass back in hours, so a reviewer starts from
what needs their attention rather than from a blank sheet.

When the applicant resubmits, the next cycle shows what changed and what did not — so a reviewer
picks up where they left off instead of starting over.

## Bring other departments in without chasing them

Fire and EMS see the fire findings. Public Works sees theirs. Zoning, structural and engineering
the same. Each department works its own scope at the same time rather than queuing behind the
others, which is where most of the calendar time actually goes.

You can see where an application is sitting and how long it has been there. And the applicant
receives **one consolidated letter** instead of three arriving on different days.

## See the tax picture in one place

Hotel occupancy filings, what was reported, what exemptions were claimed, and how the period
compares with the last twelve months — reconciled against what the city actually booked, with any
difference shown and explained rather than blended into a single revenue figure.

Identifying who has **not** filed is the natural next question and the more valuable one. Whether
this feed can answer it depends on what the source system provides, which is an open question with
the vendor and is scoped separately.

## Find anything, inside the documents

Search reads the **text inside** your PDFs, not just filenames. Type "drainage easement" and you
get the plan sheet, the council packet and the budget summary that mention it, with the sentence
shown.

It also tells you what it could not read — for example that seven documents in a folder are
scanned images with no text layer — so you know when to go look by hand rather than assume there
is nothing there.

## Keep a submittal's history straight

When an applicant sends a revised drawing, it becomes **version 2 of the same document**, not a
second file next to the first. One record, every revision, in order.

Every document also records who provided it, when, and in what capacity — so "who gave us this
and when" is answerable months later.

## One system, and each department sees its own part

Overview, Development Services, Finance, Public Works, Parks, Police, Fire and EMS, Fleet and a
public Citizen view all sit behind one navigation and one login.

Access is by department. Finance sees Finance. Development Services sees theirs. The City Manager
sees across all of it. Sign-in adds a second step for security.

Your staff learn one system. New staff learn it once.

## Answer "where did that come from?" in the room

Every figure carries the system it came from and the date it was read. When a council member asks
about a number, the answer is on the screen rather than a follow-up email.

## It works before everything is connected

Connecting a city's systems takes time. Each page shows what it does, what it still needs, and
what connecting one more source would fill in — so progress is visible from the first week rather
than the last.

---

## Resident information stays protected

Residents' names and phone numbers stay out of the lists staff scan all day. And there is a record
of who has looked at which resident's record — a question that cannot be answered today.

---

## About these screens

Every figure on these screens is sample data, shown so the layout and behaviour can be reviewed
before anything is built. Screens showing a map, a drainage model or a plan sheet are marked as
placeholder illustrations.
