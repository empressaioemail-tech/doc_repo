# Adversarial review: the verified franchise economy

## Verdict

The concept is two businesses wearing one name, and only one of them survives this review. The member layer plus the verification layer (fan twins, athlete twins, venue twins, the consumptive platform built for the Bulls) is a genuine, cheap, defensible expression of the referent-layer thesis, and it works with the token turned off. The capital layer and the RWA enhancer do not survive contact with the evidence the portfolio itself gathered on 2026-08-13. The capital token has no identified external buyer whose demand is not a bet on the token itself; it is proposed to be issued by the single most fragile counterparty in the portfolio's history (a startup arena league eleven months old at signing, in a sport that is a graveyard of folded leagues, whose own contract contemplates its sale); the "AF1 is issuer of record, we keep the IP" seam is negotiation-unstable in one direction and liability-porous in the other; and the stadium-funding claim asks token liquidity to do something no token market has ever done at any scale, in a category where the flagship deal trades at zero volume. The thesis also quietly reverses a load-bearing sentence of ratified canon (doc 42: "We build no token layer; we build stubs") without a decision record. Recommendation up front: keep the Bulls member economy and the verification lane, keep the general franchise-economy abstraction as a paper asset, and treat the center-issued capital token and the token-funds-stadiums leg as unproven third-party territory we attest for but do not design, operate, or depend on. That is where doc 42 already drew the seam, and the seam was right.

## Findings, ranked by severity

### F1. The capital layer's demand is self-referential (severity: critical)

The attack. Ask whose money enters the capital layer and what they get. Fans are already served by the member layer, which is deliberately consumptive and non-tradable; a fan who buys the sub-token is by definition buying something the fan layer does not give them, which is profit exposure. But the sub-token confers no claim on team cash flows (3.1(a) walls off team equity; that is the entire reason the token is linked to league-owned brand and territory instead). A tradable instrument linked to "brand and territory" of a twelve-team startup arena league, with no cash-flow rights, is worth what the next buyer believes, which is the definition of a reflexive asset. The economy underneath does not need the token: doc 10's honest magnitude statement is tens of thousands of dollars in the founding window on a 2,000-contact list. So the token is not financing the member economy and the member economy is not backing the token. The token points at the member economy for narrative, and the member economy points at the token for a valuation story. That is a loop, not a market.

The evidence. The illustrative numbers make this concrete. The financial one-pager shows roughly $350K per team per digital season and $4M+ across twelve franchises, explicitly not-a-projection. Take it at face value anyway: $4M gross digital revenue across an entire league is less than the cost of running the platform, the tokenomics apparatus (supply design, listings, market-making, treasury, legal wrapper, continuous attestation infrastructure), and the compliance overhead the capital layer drags in. And face value is generous: $350K per team is roughly ten times the honest founding-window magnitude doc 10 states for the live team, in a league where the entire franchise fee is $200K. Meanwhile the direct comparables all point one way. Chiliz, with 70+ partner organizations and real brands, watched CHZ fall roughly 47 percent in a 30-day window in June 2026 while World Cup fan tokens sold off through the group stage (Pass A). Watford, a century-old club with Premier League history, raised about 20 percent of its tokenized-equity target (Pass A/B). St. Regis Aspen, the flagship tokenized real asset, trades at zero daily volume eight years after issuance (Pass B). Every one of these had more underlying demand than an AF1 sub-token will.

What would have to be true. A named category of buyer must exist whose purchase is not a bet on token price: a sponsor who needs the token to buy inventory it cannot buy in dollars, an institution buying a structured claim on league revenue that the league can legally sell, or a fan segment demonstrably willing to pay for tradability itself at scale. None is currently named. "The tokenomics team will design demand" is not an answer; tokenomics allocates demand, it does not create it.

Mitigation. Run the member economy for a full Bulls season with the token turned off and measure what the sponsorship and premium layers actually earn. If a capital instrument is ever justified, the honest first instrument is the one doc 09 already identified: a revenue-share note against team local revenue, boring, legal, and pointed at real cash flow. A note that cannot sell is the cheapest possible proof that the token could not have either.

### F2. AF1 as master issuer is concentration risk on the portfolio's weakest counterparty (severity: critical)

The attack. The corrected architecture makes AF1 the issuer and governor of the master token, with every per-team sub-token hanging off it. That stakes the entire capital structure on the continuity of an entity formed September 6, 2024, whose members buy in for $200,000, in a sport where league failure is the base rate, not the tail risk. Arena football's own history (multiple AFL collapses), Austin's own history (Wranglers and Wild both folded), and a same-metro same-season competitor (ATX Draft, IFL, spring 2027) are all in the portfolio's research record. If the league folds, the master token's linked assets (league brand, territory grants) evaporate and every sub-token holder becomes a plaintiff with Empressa's name in the operating documents. If the league contracts, sub-tokens of dead franchises become orphaned instruments in a live token family, poisoning the rest. And if the league is sold, the Purchase Agreement already shows how AF1 thinks about that: the 1.3 buyout clause extinguishes member revenue share at a formula price on sale. An acquirer takes the brand and territory assets the token is "linked" to, with no obligation to token holders unless counsel builds one, and a token whose linkage the issuer can sell out from under it is not linked to anything.

The evidence. Doc 09 (formation date, $200K membership, LOC and capital-call structure, league-sale buyout at a 50/30/20 weighted average of three years' revenue); Pass B finding 5 (competitor and folded predecessors); the general arena football record.

What would have to be true. Either the master token's asset linkage must survive league death and league sale (which requires the linked assets to sit in a bankruptcy-remote structure the league does not control, at which point the league is not really the issuer), or the model must be worth building for a counterparty with a meaningful probability of not existing in 36 months. Neither is currently true.

Mitigation. Invert the architecture. Prove per-node member economies with no center-issued instrument; if a capital layer is ever added, add it per-node against assets the node controls, so one node's failure strands one instrument, not the family. Treat the AF1 master token as a design exercise for the template, not a thing to be built at AF1. The flagship logo is not worth being structurally long the flagship's survival.

### F3. "Issuer of record without the IP" is not a stable deal, and the liability boundary will not hold (severity: critical)

The attack. The design-partner offer asks AF1 to carry the securities liability of issuing a tradable capital instrument while Empressa keeps the model, the template, and every refinement, plus platform economics. Interrogate both directions. If AF1 is sophisticated enough to issue a counsel-structured security responsibly, it is sophisticated enough to notice it is being offered liability without equity in the thing generating it, and the "never owns the model" term dies in the first real negotiation. If AF1 is not sophisticated enough to notice, it is not a safe issuer to stand behind an instrument our name is all over. There is no third case. In the other direction, the "we just build the platform" boundary does not survive an adversary. A plaintiff or regulator will see one party that designed the token economy, operates the rails it runs on, markets the drops, meters the flows, and takes a fee on every transaction. That fact pattern reads as promoter and seller conduct, not vendor conduct: solicitation-based seller liability, participation liability, and state blue-sky claims do not stop at the entity that signed the offering documents. The BIG3 class action in the portfolio's own research is the template, and it was brought by private plaintiffs, meaning the friendly-SEC posture is irrelevant to it.

The evidence. Doc 42a (design-partner terms); doc 13 execution note (our lane versus the tokenomics team's lane, with Empressa operating rails, referent layer, and fan graph); Pass A finding on BIG3 (ownership-flavored marketing without equity as private-plaintiff bait independent of SEC posture); Pass A caveat that the March 2026 guidance is conduct-driven, and the conduct here is substantially ours.

What would have to be true. Securities counsel would have to endorse, in writing, that a party who designs, operates, and profits from a token economy sits outside the liability perimeter because a league signed the issuance paperwork. That opinion is unlikely to be given, and if given, unlikely to hold.

Mitigation. Price being inside the perimeter instead of pretending to be outside it: E&O and D&O sized for securities claims, indemnity structure in the engagement contract, counsel review of every marketing surface, and a walk-away rule if AF1 will not fund its own securities counsel. Separately, resolve the IP asymmetry honestly: either the partner gets a license with real economics (and the "never owns the model" line softens) or the pitch should expect to lose the negotiation on that term and decide now what the fallback is.

### F4. The three-lane regulatory discipline blurs at every real touch point (severity: high)

The attack. On paper the lanes are clean: consumptive member layer, securities-by-design capital layer, RWA enhancer. In practice the lanes cross at exactly the transactions the model needs to work. A fan who buys the sub-token has crossed from lane one to lane two, and the marketing that moved them (drops, team content, stadium dreams) becomes evidence of profit expectation attached to a retail sale. A sponsor who pays in the token makes it a payment instrument, a third characterization. A player paid or bonused in the token becomes an investor in his own employer's league, layering labor law and the league's central-payroll assessment structure onto securities law. And the RWA enhancer is a standing profit narrative for the whole token family: "capital-layer liquidity funds center-owned stadiums" is the sentence a plaintiff's exhibit list is built from, and it cannot be quarantined to the capital lane because it is the headline of the pitch. The guidance that makes lane one safe explicitly warns that communications creating profit expectations flip the analysis, the analysis is conduct-driven, CLARITY has not passed, and the entire friendly posture is interpretive, meaning one administration or one enforcement-priority memo from reversal.

The evidence. Pass A findings 1 and 2 (guidance caveat verbatim; CLARITY unpassed as of 2026-08-13); doc 13 corrected architecture (the stadium-funding narrative is structural, not incidental); doc 10 (player payout mechanics already flagged as touching tax and league assessment questions).

What would have to be true. The lanes hold only if the capital instrument is never marketed on member surfaces, members are never cross-sold into it, the token is never used as a payment or compensation rail, and the RWA narrative is confined to accredited-investor materials. That is a discipline the model's own growth logic will constantly pressure, because the member graph is the obvious distribution channel and the pitch already fuses the layers.

Mitigation. Write the firewall rules now, as product constraints, not marketing guidelines: separate surfaces, separate entities, no token acceptance on the member rail, no player compensation in the instrument, accredited-gating on every capital-layer surface. If the tokenomics team resists any of these, that is diagnostic.

### F5. Token liquidity cannot fund a venue, and the sequencing claim is inverted (severity: high)

The attack. The RWA leg claims capital-layer liquidity funds league-owned stadiums at $60M to $150M per venue. Work the arithmetic. Extracting even $60M from a token market without collapsing it requires a deep, liquid market cap several multiples larger, plausibly in the high hundreds of millions, for the token of a league whose twelve franchises paid $2.4M in combined fees and whose illustrative league-wide digital economy is $4M a year. There is no path from those numbers to that market cap that is not pure speculative inflation, and building a real asset on speculative inflation is how the instrument and the asset fail together. The precedent record is unanimous: no tokenized stadium or arena has ever been completed anywhere; construction-phase tokenization in practice is debt-shaped and accredited-only; the largest fan-financed construction raises deliver hundreds of thousands to low millions per campaign; the one genuinely large fan raise (Packers, $65.8M) is a grandfathered nonprofit selling a certificate, not a security that could fund a private league's asset.

The evidence. Pass B findings 1 through 4 and the venue cost reality section; Pass B's own conclusion that the tokenized tranche should be presumed debt or revenue-participation shaped and that fan raises are "a slice, never the building."

What would have to be true. Either the token market cap fantasy materializes and sustains through a multi-year construction cycle, or "token funds stadiums" quietly means "conventional capital stack (municipal participation, senior debt, sponsor naming) with a small token-wrapped community junior tranche," which is defensible but is not the pitch.

Mitigation. Re-sequence honestly: venue financing is conventional-first, with the twin as the disclosure and verification layer (genuinely novel, genuinely ours) and an optional community tranche in the low single-digit millions. Sell the twin-as-continuous-disclosure to the lenders and the municipality; that is a real product this portfolio can deliver today. Drop "liquidity funds the stadium" from every artifact before counsel sees it, because it is also an F4 exhibit.

### F6. The human-twin layer has consent asymmetries and failure modes the thesis has not priced (severity: medium-high)

The attack. The athlete layer's consent story assumes a free choice that is not free. AF1 players are paid at league-set levels through central payroll; supplemental twin income is explicitly positioned as the recruiting lever. An athlete "consenting" to his employer's monetization platform, where declining plausibly costs roster standing and the pitch to join the team is the platform itself, is consent under economic dependence, and it will be characterized that way in any dispute. The hard cases are unaddressed: a cut player whose premium followers are mid-subscription, an injured player whose "injury-return states told honestly" content is now adverse employment information published by his employer's vendor, a player in a contract dispute whose earning twin the team platform controls, a deceased or disgraced player whose twin has paying followers. The portable-record promise (tenant-shared, athlete owns their side) is the right answer and is currently a sentence, not a build. Separately, even though the athlete layer is deliberately non-tradable, it sits one click away from a tradable sub-token family in the same brand, and the creator-coin history (person-linked tokens, pump-and-abandon, the general ugliness of pricing humans) means the "human twins plus tokens" combination invites a headline the design does not deserve but will get.

The evidence. Doc 09 (central payroll via assessments); doc 10 (recruiting positioning, injury-state content, payout mechanics flagged open, portable record as promise); general creator-coin record.

What would have to be true. Consent must be genuinely severable from employment (opt-out with no roster consequence, in writing, and believed), and the cut/injured/dispute/death states must have designed answers before the first athlete signs, not after the first incident.

Mitigation. An athlete-rights charter shipped with the A1 program: opt-out safe harbor, revenue continuation rules on cut or injury, dispute escrow, estate handling, and athlete-side data export from day one. This is cheap now and impossible to retrofit credibly after a bad case. It is also a real differentiator, which is the only reason to believe it gets built.

### F7. The thesis reverses ratified canon without a decision record, and half of it is diworsification (severity: medium-high)

The attack. Doc 42, canonical and ratified, says verbatim: "We build no token layer; we build stubs," and places the portfolio as the verified-truth feed that RWA issuers plug into, explicitly not the issuer's designer or operator. Doc 42a stands up a tokenomics team to design the capital instrument of a partner economy Empressa operates end to end. That is a posture reversal on a load-bearing canon sentence, and it is currently recorded as a framing addition in a stub thesis, not a decision record with reversal criteria. On focus: the portfolio's live spine is the Texas flush launch gate, Factory 1.5, and the Smart Site GTM, and the focus-queue rule requires naming what gets queued to make room. Nothing has been named. Now the strongest version of each side. Kill it: the member platform reuses the portfolio's rails, but the capital layer reuses nothing; token supply design, listings, treasury, and market-making are a new business (capital-markets structuring) in which the portfolio has zero edge, pointed at the weakest counterparty available, during the exact quarter the Texas launch gate needs every cycle; this is the definition of diworsification wearing the thesis's vocabulary. Cohering: the fan twin, athlete twin, and venue twin are the referent layer meeting a new vertical; the franchise-economy abstraction (center, nodes, members, anchored assets) is genuinely the same shape as SmartCity and the catalog; the Bulls work is cheap, live, and proves the member economy with real money; and every partner economy deposits verification demand back into the substrate, which is the spine rule satisfied. Which is more convincing: the cohering case is true of the member and verification layers and false of the capital and RWA layers. The abstraction coheres; the instrument does not. The thesis's error is bundling them so that the coherent half smuggles the incoherent half past the spine rule.

The evidence. Doc 42 market-posture section (OWN public, CONNECT to private RWA, "we're the truth-underneath, not the issuer"); doc 42a section "Built off what exists today" (the tokenomics team as a forming in-house function); CLAUDE.md focus-queue rule; the current OPS-16 plan of record's Texas scope.

What would have to be true. Nick ratifies the canon reversal explicitly, with a decision record naming reversal criteria and naming what gets queued. Absent that, doc 42a is a stub thesis in conflict with ratified canon, and canon wins.

Mitigation. Split the thesis at the seam doc 42 drew: member economy and verification layer proceed as the Bulls engagement and the franchise-platform product; capital instrument design stays outside the boundary (the tokenomics team as an external partner or the partner's own vendor, with Empressa selling attestation into it). That keeps the flywheel, the recurring revenue, and the substrate deposits, and sheds the reflexivity, the issuer entanglement, and the canon conflict in one cut.

### F8. Verification is a moat for the layer worth keeping and no moat for the layer at risk (severity: medium)

The attack. Chiliz is MiCA-licensed, has 70+ partner organizations, claims over $700M delivered to sports partners, entered the US in 2026 through the Playfly college deal, and is literally named in the SEC guidance as the example of a compliant fan token. If the Verified Franchise pitch works on any league, Chiliz can counter-pitch that league with a live platform, a licensed rail, and a track record, at a price a startup league finds easier to believe. DraftKings and Fanatics already own the fan wallet and the licensing relationships one tier up. Against that field, "verification" is a real differentiator only where the buyer prices truth: athlete performance claims with evidence, venue state as continuous disclosure, sponsor inventory priced on a consented verified graph. Fan-token buyers demonstrably price fandom and momentum, not attestation; nothing in the Chiliz record suggests verified linkage claims move token demand at all. So the moat is real exactly where this review says the value is (the referent layer, the member economy, the disclosure product) and decorative exactly where the thesis wants to expand (the tradable capital layer).

The evidence. Pass A fan-token landscape; the March 2026 guidance's naming of Socios; Pass B platform landscape (Securitize, tZERO and their patent litigation as vendor risk on the issuance stack we would depend on anyway).

What would have to be true. For verification to moat the capital layer, token buyers or regulators would have to demand continuous attestation as a condition of the instrument. The only plausible route is regulatory (attestation as disclosure requirement), which is an argument for selling attestation to issuers and regulators, not for being the issuer's designer.

Mitigation. Position against Chiliz explicitly as the layer they do not have: they run token rails, we make the claims underneath verifiable. That positioning only works if we are not also trying to be the token rail, which is one more reason to cut at the F7 seam.

## The five questions Nick must answer before spending more

1. Name the third buyer: who, specifically and not hypothetically, puts money into the capital token other than fans upgraded from the member layer and parties we or the league control, and what do they hold the day AF1 folds or is sold under its own buyout clause?

2. Will you accept, in writing and priced into the engagement (insurance, indemnity, counsel), that Empressa sits inside the securities-liability perimeter of any economy it designs and operates, or is the program contingent on a carve-out opinion counsel actually signs?

3. Is doc 42's "we build no token layer" reversed? If yes, write the decision record with reversal criteria and name the live PLAN-ROW work that gets queued to fund the tokenomics and franchise build; if no, the capital layer moves outside the boundary and the thesis gets re-cut.

4. What does the Bulls member economy earn in the founding window with the token turned off, and is that number, times a realistic node count, a business you would build with no capital layer attached? If the honest answer is no, the token is load-bearing and F1 applies in full.

5. Write the one sentence a sub-token buyer would be told about what they own if AF1 is sold or dissolved within 24 months. If that sentence cannot be written today without counsel flinching, the master-token architecture is not ready to be pitched to anyone, including AF1.
