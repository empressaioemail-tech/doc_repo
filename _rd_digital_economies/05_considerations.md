# Considerations

## Regulatory

1. **The three-lane discipline is load-bearing and fragile at the edges.** The lanes are clean on paper; the blur cases must be designed for explicitly: a fan who buys the sub-token (they may; it is a public instrument — but no member surface may ever market it), a sponsor paying in tokens, a player compensated in tokens (avoid until counsel rules), member perks granted to token holders (dangerous: it welds the lanes; presumption against).
2. **The friendly posture is interpretive, not statutory.** The March 2026 guidance can be re-read by a future administration; CLARITY is unpassed. Design so the member lane survives any regime and the capital lane's wrapper can tighten (to full registration) without rebuilding the economy.
3. **Our own posture**: designing, operating, and profiting from the whole economy while the center is issuer-of-record needs a counsel-drawn boundary early (who is statutory issuer, who is technology vendor, where promoter liability lands). This is the platform's biggest own-risk item.
4. State-level: money transmission (custodial accounts), Texas biometric law (athlete capture), state blue sky on the capital lane.

## Economic

1. **Reflexivity is the killer of every predecessor.** Hold rule 3 (solvent with the token off) as an invariant with the same status as honest-absence. The token layer buys assets, never payroll.
2. **Thin liquidity is the base case**, not a risk case. Fan-token volumes and the flagship RWA token's zero-volume reality are the reference points. Design for illiquid-but-honest rather than promising markets that will not exist.
3. **The center is a startup.** The first design partner is a 2024-formed league in a sport with a history of folded leagues. The master token must be designed for network contraction, fold, and sale scenarios from day one (the network's own documents contemplate a sale). Concentration risk argues for getting the model, not just the first partner, right.
4. **Platform economics must be honest**: the tokenomics apparatus (design, counsel, treasury ops, market-making, compliance) has real fixed costs; at twelve small nodes the member lane alone may not carry them. The model must show the break-even network size.

## Human (the part we care most about getting right)

1. **Person-linked value has an ugly history** (creator coins, social tokens). Our design differs in kind: athlete records are non-tradable, value routes to the person, consent is self-custodied. Hold that line absolutely; the moment a person's twin becomes a tradable object, we have rebuilt the thing that failed and deserved to.
2. **Consent under employment power**: an athlete "consenting" to their employer's platform is not free consent by default. Mitigations: per-data-class consent, athlete-side visibility (see exactly what fans see), revocation with effect, the earning ledger visible from day one, and the portable record they keep after leaving. The locker room must experience the twin as theirs.
3. **Exit states**: cut, injured, disputed, deceased. Each needs a designed answer for the twin, its content, its accruals, and its links before season one, not after the first hard case.
4. **Members are people too**: the fan graph is a consented marketing asset, not a surveillance asset; per-class consent and no dark patterns are the standing rules.

## Strategic

1. **Focus**: this must remain an expression of the referent-layer thesis (it is: twins, attestations, rails, containers — all existing machinery pointed at a new economy shape), not a new company bolted on. The test: every dollar spent here should harden machinery the rest of the portfolio uses.
2. **Competitive**: Chiliz (US-entering, licensed, incumbent) can sell any league fan tokens tomorrow. They cannot sell verified athletes, born-twin venues, disclosure rooms, or the RWA lane — verification is the moat if we keep it load-bearing, and just a feature if we let the token be the story.
3. **Naming and brand**: "we build digital economies" is a strong category claim and a big promise; ratify deliberately (decision-log), and keep Hauska substrate-only per canon.
4. **Partner incentive stability**: the issuer-of-record carries liability while we keep the IP. That asymmetry must be priced into partner economics or it becomes the deal's unraveling thread; the adversarial review (07) is tasked on it.
