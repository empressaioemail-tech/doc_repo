Forward-looking synthesis: turning emerging data into graph context

How new, just-happened data from a source gets matched to the entities we already track, becomes forward-looking context on them, and then flips into history and trains the system.

Naming, so it is precise: there is no "forward-looking node." A source (news, EDGAR, a legislature docket) lives in the source registry. Emerging data becomes an event node with anticipatory atoms (a future valid_from + a probability). A would_affect edge links it to the existing subject nodes it could matter to. "Forward-looking" is a property of those atoms and edges, not a kind of node.
Top band = the emerging, unstructured world  ·  bottom band = the structured graph we already hold  ·  they meet at RESOLVE, the synthesis step.

RESOLVE is the whole trick. Raw emerging data is useless until it is connected to an entity we track. The resolver answers "what is this about," matching the item to existing subject nodes (or minting new ones). That match, plus the would_affect edge, is the synthesis: it fuses new, unstructured signal into the structured graph of facts we already hold. Emerging data that resolves to nothing we track is noted but not forced onto the graph.

Today's pending item is tomorrow's historical fact. The same atom that warned you about something forward-looking becomes, once it resolves, the explanation in the record, and its outcome trains the two ledgers so the next emerging item of that kind is scored more accurately. Capture has to start early, because anything not captured can never be explained or learned from later.

What this shows

The system is not two separate things, a database of facts and a news reader. It is one graph that the emerging world continuously feeds into. New data arrives unstructured, gets resolved onto the entities we already track, hangs there as forward-looking context with a probability and a citation, and then, when it resolves, settles into history and trains the prediction. Facts, atoms, nodes, and emerging data are all the same fabric, just at different stages of the same lifecycle.
